# Gnosis Must Own the Decision Record

Gnosis becomes clinical and industry-leading by narrowing its decision space to the decisions regulators already fine firms for failing to record (fair-value marks, liquidity tools, trade allocation, model changes, ESG screen application) plus the high-volume, rationale-free decisions where peer-reviewed evidence shows value destruction (exits, sizing, corporate-action elections), and by making each committed record carry the field set of MiFID Delegated Regulation Article 74, Advisers Act Rule 204-2(a)(3) and the Rule 17a-4 audit-trail alternative, with an OMS order-ID link, versioned policy text and hash-chained integrity evidence. Its UI needs to look like J.P. Morgan's Salt and IBM's Carbon rather than consumer SaaS: medium density by default with a documented high-density opt-in, lining tabular figures, a five-state status scale that never relies on colour alone, a maker-checker state machine with frozen payloads, two labelled clocks for replay, a check-answers seal page, AI labels that drop on human edit, a visible tool trace, and cognitive forcing (record your own view before the recommendation is revealed) on gated decisions. The metrics to beat are not the vendors' unaudited alpha claims (Essentia 150 bps/yr, Cabot more than 150 bps, Alpha Theory +3.9%/yr) but the numbers nobody publishes: **100% of committed decisions with a sealed, attributable rationale**, **sub-hour retrieval of any decision's full record** against the SEC's 10-to-14-day document-production window, **100% deterministic replay hash-match**, hallucination under 1% on template-bound output, and capital-markets retention (GRR at or above 95%, NRR at or above 110%). The most saleable path is a CCO-plus-COO sale into US SEC-registered advisers in the USD 5 to 50bn band, wedged on AI-governance decision lineage and investment-committee decision capture (not guideline exceptions, which the OMS already owns), priced as a platform fee plus decision-type modules and approver seats (never AUM basis points), proven through two or three discounted design partnerships and a 60-to-90-day single-desk pilot, and protected against the one fatal failure mode: entering InfoSec review without a SOC 2 Type II, an AITEC-AIMA DDQ and a DORA Article 30 contract pack.

## Regulators already fine firms for five unrecorded decision types

The decision space gnosis should model is not a blank taxonomy of everything a buy-side firm decides. The 2020 to 2026 enforcement record shows regulators treating a specific subset of decisions as "must be recorded with rationale, actor and time", and penalising the absence of exactly the record gnosis's commit event is designed to produce. Western Asset Management paid a **USD 100m civil penalty** in 2024 for cherry-picking: trading began as early as 5:00 am Pacific but allocation instructions were routinely entered after noon, so first-day gains could be steered to favoured portfolios ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/mutual-funds/western-asset-agrees-to-100m-sec-penalty-over-cherry-picking-scheme/266892); [SEC complaint](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2024/comp-pr2024-187.pdf)) [V]. The SEC's questioning of the former co-CIO hinged on whether he "had an allocation in mind" when placing trades, and he pleaded guilty in June 2026 to obstructing the probe ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/kenneth-leech-pleads-guilty-to-obstructing-cherry-picking-probe.html)) [V]. Two Sigma paid **USD 90m** in January 2025 after employees identified model vulnerabilities in or before March 2019 that were not addressed until August 2023, with researchers holding read/write model access without oversight ([SEC 2025-15](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15)) [V]. Macquarie paid **USD 79.8m** for valuing roughly 4,900 illiquid odd-lot CMOs at institutional-lot prices ([SEC 2024-140](https://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-140)) [V], and Cleary characterises valuation as a continuing 2026 enforcement priority after the Sciens and Madison Capital cases ([Cleary Enforcement Watch](https://www.clearyenforcementwatch.com/2026/03/enforcers-target-fund-valuation-practices/)) [V]. In the UK, the FCA's July 2025 decision notices fined Neil Woodford **GBP 5.9m** and Woodford Investment Management **GBP 40m** for liquidity-risk failures ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-over-woodford-equity-income-fund)) [V], two years after its 14-firm liquidity review found "insufficient challenge and escalation" in governance ([FCA Dear CEO letter](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/correspondence/dear-ceo-letter-liquidity-management-multi-firm-review.pdf)) [V]. ESG screen application produced **USD 19m** (DWS), **USD 4m** (Goldman Sachs AM) and **USD 1.5m** (BNY Mellon IA) penalties for failing to evidence that the stated process was actually applied ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/deutsche-dws-25-million-esg-aml-penalty-sec-fixler-hoops-wohrmann-misstatement-goldman-bny/694709/)) [V]. Above all of these sits the off-channel communications sweep: roughly **USD 2.7bn across about 60 firms** since 2021 ([Carlton Fields](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/expect-focus/2024/sec-penalties-for-off-channel-communications)) [V], with the first standalone adviser settlement (USD 6.5m) in April 2024 ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/04/sec-files-first-charges-against-standalone-investment-adviser)) [V], which confirms that advice-related discussion routinely lives in unretained messaging.

The common thread is that regulators penalise the absence of a record binding **intent, timing and approver** [I]: allocation intent fixed at order entry, who changed a model and who approved it, why a mark was chosen and by whom, and who challenged a liquidity decision. That is precisely the tuple a sealed commit with actor, UTC timestamp, rationale and approval chain produces. The legal hooks are explicit. For US advisers, Rule 204-2(a)(3) requires a memorandum of each order identifying the person who recommended the transaction and the person who placed it ([eCFR 275.204-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.204-2)) [P]. For EU portfolio managers, Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Article 74 requires the firm to record immediately, for every "decision to deal", the person making the decision, the date and time, the instrument, direction, quantity and price ([EUR-Lex 2017/565](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0565)) [P], and AIFMD Level 2 Article 64 and UCITS Directive 2010/43 Article 14 mirror it ([legislation.gov.uk 231/2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2013/231/contents)) [V]. Article 74 is the single most important EU hook for gnosis, and also its most likely shortfall: the commit event is that record only if its schema carries the Annex IV Section 1 fields or links unambiguously to the OMS record that does [I].

### Two evidence classes define the priority order

The behavioural evidence points at a different set of decisions from the enforcement evidence, and the product needs both. Akepanidtaworn, Di Mascio, Imas and Schmidt, in the *Journal of Finance* (December 2023), find "clear evidence of skill in buying" while selling decisions "underperform substantially", even relative to random selling, because sells are "driven by heuristics rather than information" ([Wiley JoF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13271); [NBER w29076](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29076)) [V qualitative; the bps magnitude was not captured]. Inalytics' database, described as USD 22 trillion of trades over 20 years, reports an average hit rate of **49.6%** and an average win/loss ratio of **102%** ([WealthBriefingAsia](https://www.wealthbriefingasia.com/article.php/Fund-Managers-Wrong-More-Often-Than-Not-_dash_-Research)) [V; n not disclosed], with **88%** of PMs showing research skill but only **46%** delivering positive alpha from sizing ([MRM/Inalytics](https://www.mrm-london.com/whats-happening/press/research-process-stock-picking-the-dominant-source-of-alpha-in-88-of-institutional-portfolios-finds-new-research-by-inalytics/)) [V]. Essentia's Behavioral Alpha Benchmark at 31 December 2025 shows a median score of **47.7** across **163 PMs** and only **39.3%** of portfolios adding value through decision-making ([Essentia Q4 2025](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-q4-2025/)) [V]. None of these decision types (exit, sizing, add or trim) has a regulatory record standard or, under a Capital Group-style model where "there are no group decisions" ([Capital Group](https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/about-us/capital-system.html)) [V], a committee gate. So the product's case for them is performance feedback, while for valuation, liquidity, allocation and model changes the case is defensibility [I]. Corporate-action elections sit in a third position: DTCC headlines the industry cost at **USD 58bn** ([DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/teamsport)) [V, breakdown not captured], Charles River already sells "four eyes verification on elections with audit trails" ([Charles River Corporate Actions](https://info.crd.com/corporate-actions)) [V], and no per-election error rate was found anywhere.

### What a complete record contains, according to the frameworks and the cases

Six decision-quality frameworks supply distinct, mappable concepts, and the notes source each. Spetzler's decision-quality chain has six links (appropriate frame; creative alternatives; meaningful, reliable information including uncertainty; clear values and trade-offs; sound reasoning; commitment to action) and holds that a decision's quality equals its weakest link ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/carl-spetzler-the-six-elements-of-a-good-decision/)) [V]. Kahneman, Sibony and Sunstein's *Noise* contributes the noise audit and the Mediating Assessments Protocol, which "separates evaluation into independent components before combining them into a final decision" ([The Uncertainty Project](https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/the-mediating-assessments-protocol)) [V]. Klein's pre-mortem rests on 1989 research finding that prospective hindsight raises the ability to identify reasons for future outcomes by **30%** ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)) [V]. Annie Duke's "resulting" and the decision journal separate decision quality from outcome quality ([Crucible](https://readcrucible.com/articles/decision-journals-the-practice-the-science-the-templates)) [V]; Mauboussin adds base rates and reference classes ([Alliance for Decision Education](https://alliancefordecisioneducation.org/podcasts/episode-001-base-rates-and-bees-michael-mauboussin/)) [V]. Bridgewater's Dot Collector displays both the equal-weighted and the believability-weighted vote alongside each person's vote, and flags "nubby questions" where the answer pattern reveals an important disagreement ([Principles](https://www.principles.com/principles/3290232e-6bca-4585-a4f6-66874aefce30)) [V]. Baillie Gifford's investment notes follow a fixed question framework (Edge, Growth, Sustainability) ([Baillie Gifford LTGG](https://www.bailliegifford.com/literature-library/institutional-only-literature/philosophy-and-process/long-term-global-growth-philosophy-and-process/)) [V], which is a working example of schema-driven capture per decision type; Wellington and Two Sigma publish nothing on IC mechanics that the notes could find.

Combining the journal fields with what regulators demanded in the cases yields a record with three layers [I]. The journal layer holds situation, alternatives considered (including "do nothing"), decision, rationale, expected outcome with a stated probability, a review date, kill criteria and a pre-mortem. The governance layer holds decider, approvers, each approver's independent view recorded before aggregation (MAP), weighted dissent (Bridgewater), policy checks and exceptions, and the timestamp of intent. The evidence layer holds inputs and sources with as-of timestamps, methodology and model version, third-party inputs, linked precedents and base rate, and the change log. Gnosis's schema-driven capture, policy evaluation, chained approvals, precedent linking and sealed commit map onto this cleanly; the gaps are an explicit alternatives field, a stated probability on every proposal, a pre-commit "how could this fail" prompt, a review date with a process-versus-outcome post-mortem, and per-approver independent views captured before the chain reveals others' votes [I]. Those five additions are cheap and they convert the ledger from an audit artefact into the noise-audit instrument the *Noise* authors describe: if several PMs face the same decision type with the same inputs, the variance in their marks or sizing is measurable [I].

## Audit-ready has a legal definition, and bitemporal is not in it

No regulator found by the notes names "bitemporal" as best practice; regulators define the outcome, not the technique [V]. The outcome is stated almost identically across jurisdictions. FCA SYSC 9.1 requires that the FCA can "reconstitute each element in a clear and accurate manner and ... identify easily any changes, corrections or other amendments, and the contents of the records prior to such modifications" ([FCA Handbook SYSC 9.1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sysc9/sysc9s1)) [V]. MiFID Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Article 72(1) requires records kept so the authority can "reconstitute each key stage of the processing of each transaction", with prior contents "easily ascertained" and "not possible for the records to be otherwise manipulated or altered" ([EUR-Lex 2017/565](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0565)) [P]; AIFMD Level 2 Article 66(3) and UCITS Directive 2010/43 Article 16 mirror it [P]. In the US, the 2022 amendments to broker-dealer Rule 17a-4 added an audit-trail alternative to WORM: the system must "permit the recreation of an original record if it is modified or deleted" and maintain "a complete time-stamped audit trail", effective 3 January 2023 with a compliance date of 3 May 2023 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/investment/amendments-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-broker-dealers)) [V]. Advisers have no WORM rule of their own; Rule 204-2(g) requires records to be safeguarded from "loss, alteration or destruction" and promptly reproducible ([eCFR 275.204-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.204-2)) [P], so buyers and auditors borrow 17a-4's language [I].

To an auditor, point-in-time reconstruction therefore means three concrete tests [I]: a **state test** (show the decision, its inputs, the policy version and the approvals exactly as the firm saw them at time T), a **knowledge test** (show what the firm knew at T versus what was later corrected, with who corrected it and when), and an **integrity test** (prove nothing was altered since). Bitemporal replay answers the first two natively. The third needs cryptographic or storage-level evidence plus an independent attestation; gnosis's feature list covers the first two and only implies the third [I]. Two further conditions follow. Replay fails the state test if decisions are replayed against current policy text rather than the mandate, restricted list, model and prompt versions in force at T, so every reference dataset must be versioned [I]. And the sales claim should be "we meet Article 72(1)(b)-(c), SYSC 9.1 and 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) by construction", with bitemporality described as the mechanism rather than the standard [I].

### Four-eyes is not legally required at transaction level, which changes the pitch

The notes found no US, UK or EU rule that expressly mandates two-person approval of an investment-guideline exception at transaction level [V]. "Four eyes" is a legal requirement at entity-governance level (MiFID II Article 9(6), AIFMD Article 8(1)(c), UCITS Article 7(1)(b)) [P] and a supervisory expectation extended to fund-level decisions in Luxembourg and Malta ([Waystone](https://www.waystone.com/extension-of-four-eyes-principle-to-private-funds/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1559702/what-is-the-four-eyes-principle-that-regulated-companies-need-to-adhere-to)) [V]. Gnosis's chained maker-checker approvals and non-overridable gates therefore exceed any explicit legal requirement, and the framing should be "your policies under 206(4)-7, SYSC 6 and AIFMD Article 61 promise segregation; gnosis makes it un-bypassable and self-evidencing", not "the law requires four eyes" [I]. The hard information-barrier gate does have a precise legal design target: MAR Article 9(1)(a) protects a firm only where its arrangements "effectively ensure" that neither the decision-maker nor anyone who influenced the decision possessed inside information ([EUR-Lex MAR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)) [P]. A gate is demonstrable only if the ledger records the restricted-list version in force at T, the wall status of everyone who could influence the decision, and negative evidence that no override path exists, which means an admin audit log showing no privilege escalation plus control-test evidence in the SOC 2 report [I]. Gnosis does not itself maintain MAR Article 18 insider lists, so the gate must ingest the client's list system [I].

### The AI-governance tailwinds are real, and one of them is dead

The 2026 tailwinds are specific. The SEC's 2026 Examination Priorities say examiners will assess whether firms supervise their AI use and whether actual AI usage matches representations, so that firms claiming AI drives portfolio management "must be able to demonstrate that AI tools genuinely influence investment decisions" ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/-/media/files/insights/publications/2025/11/2026-sec-division-of-examinations-priorities.pdf)) [V]. This follows the March 2024 AI-washing settlements against Delphia (USD 225,000) and Global Predictions (USD 175,000), where Global Predictions "could not produce documents to substantiate its advertised use of AI" ([Cleary](https://www.clearyenforcementwatch.com/2024/03/sec-announces-ai-washing-cases-against-investment-advisers/)) [V]. FINRA's 2026 Regulatory Oversight Report discusses AI agents for the first time and says autonomous agents "may require novel oversight, including tracking actions and restricting system access" ([Debevoise Data Blog](https://www.debevoisedatablog.com/2025/12/11/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued-focus-on-generative-ai-and-emerging-agent-based-risks/)) [V]. In the UK there will be no dedicated AI Senior Manager Function; accountability sits with existing SMFs ([Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/global-investigations/senior-managers-liable-under-uk-regulatory-regime-decisions-made-ai/)) [V], and the Treasury Committee has asked the FCA to publish practical guidance on SM&CR accountability for AI-caused harm by the end of 2026 ([Treasury Committee](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmtreasy/684/report.html)) [V]. ESMA's May 2024 statement "expects firms to be transparent on the role of AI in investment decision-making processes" ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf)) [V]. US bank regulators replaced SR 11-7 with **SR 26-2 on 17 April 2026** ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm)) [V], and IOSCO published a 2026 final supervisory toolkit covering GenAI and agentic AI ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/05/iosco-final-report-supervisory-toolkit-for-ai-use-in-capital-markets/)) [V].

Two things gnosis must not do. It must not message against the SEC's predictive-data-analytics conflicts proposal, which was formally withdrawn on **12 June 2025** ([SEC S7-12-23](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/s7-12-23)) [V]. And it must not claim EU AI Act high-risk compliance is required: the Digital Omnibus (reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) deferred Annex III high-risk obligations to **2 December 2027** ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)) [V, confirm on EUR-Lex], and investment decision support does not appear in Annex III anyway [P]. Article 50 transparency (tell natural persons they are interacting with AI) did apply from 2 August 2026 [V], so the chat agent needs a disclosure. The honest message is "high-risk-grade logging without the high-risk burden" [I].

## Competitors score decisions after the fact; incumbents gate trades, not decisions

The competitive set splits into three tiers, and none of them occupies gnosis's layer. The behavioural-analytics specialists (Alpha Theory, Essentia, Inalytics, Cabot inside FactSet) measure decision skill from trade history after the fact. Alpha Theory describes itself as "a decision process improvement platform that helps fundamental managers implement repeatable investment processes to optimally size positions" ([Alpha Theory](https://www.alphatheory.com/)) [V]; its Alpha Engine is "a customizable rules engine built to increase discipline around position sizing" and Alpha View sends alerts when "a position size is too large or when an analyst needs to update their targets" ([Alpha Theory platform](https://www.alphatheory.com/platform)) [V]. Those are soft sizing rules, not hard information-barrier or conflict gates, and nothing in its public material describes a propose-gate-approve-commit lifecycle [I]. Essentia provides behavioural analytics on "more than $430 billion" of assets ([Essentia](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/)) [V], sells Insight in Basic, Pro and Advantage tiers with Enterprise priced "based on the number of portfolios analyzed" ([Essentia Insight Enterprise](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/essentia-news-and-media/insight-enterprise-launch/)) [V], and took a Northern Trust equity stake in September 2021 ([P&I](https://www.pionline.com/money-management/northern-trust-takes-stake-behavioral-analytics-and-consulting-firm-essentia)) [V, pre-2024]. Inalytics has compiled "the largest private peer group of institutional investor decisions globally" and its DECSIS model analyses stock picking, sizing, trading activity and holding periods ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/inalytics-launches-skills-based-data-model-manager-selection/)) [V]. Cabot, acquired by FactSet on 1 June 2021, quantifies Buy, Sell and Sizing skill ([FactSet Marketplace](https://www.factset.com/marketplace/catalog/product/reveal-and-reveal-plus-cabot-investment-technology)) [V]. The research-management tier (Bipsync, FactSet IRN, Verity, Backstop, Dynamo, Allvue, AlphaSense) has the strongest audit-trail language and the fastest AI cadence, but its unit of record is the note or document. Bipsync claims "the most complete audit trail of activity through automatic versioning, logging and archiving" mapped to Rules 204-2 and 206(4)-7 ([Bipsync compliance](https://www.bipsync.com/how-we-help/how-we-help-compliance/)) [V], and launched the Toro AI research assistant on 12 November 2025 for a client base "including 15 of the top 20 U.S. university endowments" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/11/12/3186485/0/en/Bipsync-launches-Toro-an-AI-Research-Assistant-that-puts-institutional-knowledge-at-investors-fingertips.html)) [V]. ## The metrics to beat are the ones nobody publishes

The industry's decision-quality vocabulary is already standardised around two numbers. Essentia's Behavioral Alpha Score combines a hit rate ("percentage of decisions that added value") and a payoff ratio ("ratio of value added by the average good decision to the value destroyed by the average bad decision") across seven decision types: stock picking, entry timing, sizing, scaling in, size adjusting, scaling out and exit timing ([Essentia white paper page](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-paper/); [Essentia benchmark](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/product/behavioral-alpha-benchmark/)) [V]. Inalytics frames the same two questions as hit rate and win/loss ratio ([Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/identifying-manager-skill/)) [V], and a 2015 paper argues the win-loss ratio is "less influenced by luck" ([Springer FMPM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-015-0255-3)) [V, title-level]. A higher win-loss ratio in one year is associated with **2 to 4% additional risk-adjusted return** the next year ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/inalytics-and-glg-examine-what-makes-manager-skilful/)) [V; n not disclosed]. Adopting Essentia's seven-type decomposition exactly is the cheapest way to be benchmarkable by the allocators who already read its quarterly rankings [I].

The outcome claims gnosis will be compared against all share one evidentiary weakness. Essentia says the average client "improved performance by 150 basis points per year" ([Northern Trust](https://www.northerntrust.com/what-we-do/asset-servicing/Investment-Data-Science/essentia-analytics)) [V as vendor claim, n and period undisclosed, circa 2021], reports 94 bps/yr lost to behavioural biases ([Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/essentia-analytics-data-shows-where-alpha-is-lost-and-found/)) [V], and cites a 180 bps client case ([Essentia testimonials](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/testimonials/)) [V as testimonial]. Cabot says "more than 150 basis points" is typical in the first three years ([FactSet Marketplace](https://www.factset.com/marketplace/catalog/product/reveal-and-reveal-plus-cabot-investment-technology)) [V, undated]. Alpha Theory's 2023 review reports a 92% win rate for optimal over actual sizing across 12 years, 268% versus 130% compound, and **+3.9% annualised** over 14 years across "well over 200 equity funds" ([Alpha Theory 2023 review](https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2023-year-in-review)) [V]; its 2025 review updates this to 13 of 14 years and 57% per position ([Alpha Theory 2025 review](https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2025-year-in-review-position-sizing-as-a-persistent-edge)) [V]. Alpha Theory's numbers are the most transparent, since it reports the 33% of clients per year where optimal underperforms, but the "Optimal" series is a frictionless counterfactual built from the clients' own targets [I]. None of the three has independent validation [V, absence]. Given base rates of 49.6% hit rate and 102% payoff, a single fund's year-on-year improvement is within noise; a smaller, pre-registered, auditable claim beats a larger unauditable one with allocators, and gnosis should not publish an alpha-uplift number until it has at least eight quarters and twenty funds [I].

The operational metrics closest to gnosis's core have no public benchmark at all: the share of decisions with a documented rationale, proposal-to-approval cycle time, guideline-exception turnaround and PM time spent on documentation were all "no public figure found" [V, absence]. That absence is the positioning opportunity: gnosis can define and publish the reference numbers from anonymised ledger data, in the style of Essentia's quarterly rankings [I]. The one operational yardstick every CCO already knows is the SEC's document-production window: advisers "typically have about two weeks to begin electronically submitting documents", with response periods of **10 to 14 days**, routine exams running **60 to 120 days** and deficiency responses due in 30 days ([Stratifi](https://www.stratifi.com/blog/sec-exam-preparation-investment-advisors-practical-guide); [Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/sec-examination-exam-information-request-letter-risk-alert-compliance-manual-policies-procedures-code-of-ethics/)) [V]. Turning a two-week scramble into a sub-hour query is the cleanest, most demonstrable KPI in the product [I]. On false positives, the number prospects have in their heads is AML transaction monitoring's **90 to 95%** false-positive alert rate at USD 25 to 50 of review cost per alert ([FluxForce](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/false-positive-rates-transaction-monitoring)) [V, vendor blogs citing PwC]; gnosis's gates are deterministic rules over structured proposals, so a 90% false-positive rate would indicate mis-specified policy, not the norm, and a published precision/recall makes the contrast explicit [I].

For AI-assistance quality the regulated-domain reference points are stark. Stanford RegLab's pre-registered study found Lexis+ AI hallucinated on **17%** of legal queries and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research on **33%**, against 58 to 80% for general chatbots, after both vendors had claimed hallucination-free citations ([Stanford RegLab](https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools/); [arXiv 2405.20362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20362)) [V]. Clinical decision support shows alert override rates of **46.2 to 96.2%** across 23 studies ([JMIR 2020](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15653)) [V] and a pooled **90%** override of drug-interaction alerts ([SAGE 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14604582241263242)) [V], which is the alert-fatigue regime gnosis's recommendation engine must stay out of. Vectara's grounded-summarisation leaderboard puts the best models at **0.6 to 0.7%** on its original set and **3.3%** on the harder November 2025 set, with reasoning models above 10% ([Vectara](https://www.vectara.com/blog/introducing-the-next-generation-of-vectaras-hallucination-leaderboard)) [V]; gnosis's template-bound outputs over structured ledger facts sit near the easy floor, free-form chat nearer the hard set [I]. The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard shows multi-turn tool-selection scores dropping 5 to 10 points against single-turn ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)) [V via secondary], so tool accuracy should be measured on multi-step walkthroughs [I]. For calibration, Good Judgment Project superforecasters averaged a Brier score of **0.166** against 0.259 for regular forecasters ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)) [V], and 0.124 on 41 central-bank decisions in 2024 to 2026 ([Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.com/services/futurefirst/)) [V, vendor-reported].

For reliability, no incumbent publishes an uptime SLA in any accessible source [V, absence]; DORA has applied since **17 January 2025** and the FCA's operational-resilience regime has been fully in force since **31 March 2025**, both requiring impact tolerances (and under DORA, RTO/RPO) per important service without prescribing numbers ([RegPulse](https://www.regpulse.io/blog/operational-resilience-requirements)) [V]. Because clients must write RTO/RPO into their registers, gnosis should hand them over pre-filled, and because nobody publishes an SLA, a public status page and nightly replay-hash reports beat an unpublished promise of more nines [I]. Commercially, the capital-markets bar is high: Clearwater's NRR was **108%** consolidated and **114%** core in 2025 ([CWAN 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1866368/000186636825000031/cwan-20250930.htm)) [V], FactSet's ASV retention exceeded **95%** with client retention of 91% in FY2025 ([FactSet](https://investor.factset.com/news-releases/news-release-details/factset-reports-results-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2025)) [V], and MSCI's index retention was **96.1%** ([MSCI 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1408198/000140819825000194/exhibit991earningsrelease-.htm)) [V], against private-SaaS medians of roughly 104% NDR and 88% GRR ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/net-revenue-retention-benchmarks-2026-saas-expansion-data)) [V via aggregator]. A system of record for decisions should churn like index subscriptions, because churn means losing the audit trail [I].

## Clinical UI means Salt density, two clocks and deliberate friction

The best-documented institutional design systems converge on a small set of rules, and only one of them states the trade-off numerically. J.P. Morgan's Salt ships five densities with base control heights of **20 px (high), 28 px (medium), 36 px (low) and 44 px (touch and mobile)** ([Salt size](https://github.com/jpmorganchase/salt-ds/blob/main/site/docs/foundations/size.mdx)) [V], describes high density as "ideal for data-heavy or complex applications, such as trading platforms" and then warns that "high density doesn't meet WCAG 2.2 level AA requirements, including target size", so users "must be informed about the accessibility implications prior to applying high density" and switching density "must not change the experience beyond presentation" ([Salt density](https://github.com/jpmorganchase/salt-ds/blob/main/site/docs/foundations/density.mdx)) [V]. IBM Carbon's data table ladder runs **24, 32, 40, 48 and 64 px** row heights with 14 px text ([Carbon data table](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/main/src/pages/components/data-table/style.mdx)) [V], and WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 requires pointer targets of "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels" at Level AA ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/guidelines/sc/22/target-size-minimum.html)) [V]. A defensible policy for gnosis is therefore a Salt-style medium default (28 px controls, 32 to 40 px rows) that passes WCAG 2.2 AA, with high density as an explicit per-user opt-in behind an accessibility notice [I]. That single WCAG 2.2 AA claim now satisfies all three procurement regimes, because EN 301 549 v4.1.1 (published September 2026) adopts WCAG 2.2 ([AccessibleEU](https://accessible-eu-centre.ec.europa.eu/content-corner/news/european-accessibility-standard-en-301-549-has-been-updated-2026-09-07_en)) [V] while US Section 508 still anchors to WCAG 2.0 AA ([GSA section508.gov](https://github.com/GSA/section508.gov/blob/main/_posts/2017-01-09-blog-access-board-updates-ict-requirements.md)) [V].

"Clinical" in the Bloomberg sense comes less from colour or type than from removing navigation chrome. Bloomberg's own UX group says a "tight, information-dense screen" makes "clarifying the information hierarchy challenging" and that its keyboard exposes frequent functions on physical keys ([Bloomberg UX](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/what-we-do/ux/)) [V, snippet only]; a design critic's reading is that with keyboard-primary interaction "what is visible on screen is content, not navigation" ([KlindrOS](https://klindros.com/blog/inside-the-twilight-trading-floor-the-design-system-behind-klindros)) [V, secondary]. Command palettes reached from anywhere via Ctrl/Cmd+K, with fuzzy search, are the modern equivalent ([UX Patterns for Developers](https://uxpatterns.dev/patterns/advanced/command-palette)) [V]. Numbers should use lining tabular figures via `font-variant-numeric: lining-nums tabular-nums`, which activates "figures where numbers are all of the same size, allowing them to be easily aligned like in tables" ([MDN](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/css/reference/properties/font-variant-numeric/index.md)) [V]; Salt's typography docs give no tabular-figure guidance, so this is a differentiator rather than table stakes [I]. Status colour must never carry meaning alone: Carbon requires indicators to use "at least two of the following elements: color, shape, or symbol" with "at least a 3:1 contrast between colors used for status indicators" ([Carbon status indicators](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/main/src/pages/patterns/status-indicator-pattern/index.mdx)) [V]. Gnosis's compliance states need a five-level scale (pass, info, warn, breach, hard-block) where the hard-block state differs in shape (a lock glyph) and in available actions, not only in hue [I]. Salt recommends dark mode "if you're designing an interface that will be used continuously for long periods", with all tokens identical across modes and only colours changing ([Salt modes](https://github.com/jpmorganchase/salt-ds/blob/main/site/docs/foundations/modes.mdx)) [V].

### Explanations increase acceptance regardless of correctness, so gated decisions need friction

The four canonical AI-interface guideline sets agree on the basics. Microsoft's HAX guidelines, validated with 49 practitioners assessing 20 products, include G1 "make clear what the system can do", G2 "make clear how well the system can do what it can do", G10 "scope services when in doubt" and G11 "make clear why the system did what it did" ([Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)) [V]. NIST IR 8312's four principles require explanation, meaningfulness, explanation accuracy ("correctly reflect the system's process for generating the output") and knowledge limits (the system "only operates under conditions for which it was designed") ([NISTIR 8312](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8312)) [V]. Google PAIR frames the goal as trust calibration, so users know "when to trust and when to prioritize their own judgment" ([PAIR Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/explainability-trust/)) [V]. IBM's Carbon for AI requires "an embedded AI label and explainability popover" on every AI component, and "if the user manually overrides the AI-suggested content then the component will change from the AI variant to the default variant" with a revert control ([Carbon for AI](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon-website/blob/main/src/pages/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/index.mdx)) [V]. That last rule maps directly onto validated prose: AI-drafted rationale carries the label until a human edits or approves it [I].

The empirical literature adds a hard constraint the guidelines do not. Bansal and colleagues (CHI 2021) found that "explanations increased the chance that humans will accept the AI's recommendation, regardless of its correctness" ([arXiv 2006.14779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779)) [V]. Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos (CSCW 2021) found that cognitive forcing functions (show the AI only on request, make the user decide first, or delay the AI) "significantly reduced overreliance compared to simple explainable AI approaches", while users "assigned the least favorable subjective ratings" to the most effective designs ([arXiv 2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692)) [V]. For gnosis this means the recommendation engine's verb should not be the first thing an approver sees on a gated or high-impact decision: record your own view, then reveal, applied only to gated decision types so the friction and the need-for-cognition inequality Buçinca reports stay bounded [I]. Precedent confidence should be shown as N-best with numeric similarity, and where a distribution exists, as a frequency-framed dot plot rather than a single mean, following Padilla, Kay and Hullman's finding that distributional displays outperform interval plots ([Wiley StatsRef](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118445112.stat08296)) [V, snippet]. The shown matcher weights must be the actual weights, not a post-hoc story, or the panel fails NIST's explanation-accuracy principle [I]. A 2026 survey of agent-trace provenance names the four elements a trustworthy tool trace must show: "why a tool was selected, where its arguments came from, whether its output was reliable, and how the result shaped later decisions" ([arXiv 2606.04990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990)) [V]. Since no incumbent copilot publicly documents citation rendering, doing this well is a credible differentiator [I].

### The approval and replay screens have public precedents

Banking maker-checker practice supplies the state model. The maker submits and "the system places the record in a pending state, visible to authorized reviewers but not yet active"; systems should "store the exact payload at submission time" and "never allow modification of a pending request's payload; require cancellation and re-submission instead" ([Opcito](https://www.opcito.com/blogs/maker-checker-implementation-guide-for-secure-fintech-systems)) [V]; every action "is logged with timestamps and user identities" ([XTRM](https://blog.xtrm.com/posts/maker-checker-process)) [V]. The frozen-payload rule is what makes gnosis's decision diff trustworthy: the diff compares immutable snapshots, never a live form [I]. Bitemporal databases supply the two clocks the replay UI must expose. XTDB separates "System Time" (when the database became aware of changes, immutable) from "Valid Time" (when facts are considered true in the real world), notes that auditing needs data "as we knew it at the time, without subsequent corrections", and its finance tutorial uses paired date-range sliders for the two clocks ([XTDB Time in XTDB](https://github.com/xtdb/xtdb/blob/main/docs/src/content/docs/about/time-in-xtdb.md); [XTDB finance tutorial](https://github.com/xtdb/xtdb/blob/main/docs/src/content/docs/tutorials/financial-usecase/time-in-finance.mdx)) [V]. Gnosis's bitemporal slider should become two explicit clocks labelled in plain language ("Facts as of" and "Known as of"), defaulting to now, with a persistent banner whenever either clock is not now [I]. The seal step should be a GOV.UK-style check-answers page, which "reduces errors by giving users a final opportunity to catch and fix mistakes", with a per-row Change link and a specific button label rather than "Submit" ([GOV.UK check answers](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system/blob/main/src/patterns/check-answers/index.md)) [V]. Wizards are for users who "rarely go through that process"; repeat users need "a faster alternative" ([NN/g wizards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)) [V], so the schema-driven form should be a single scrollable surface with a task-list rail, validated on field completion ([NN/g form errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/)) [V], with hard-gate hits surfacing as soon as their inputs are complete [I]. For the Three.js marketing site, React Three Fiber's guidance is to render on demand, keep draw calls "under 1,000 maximum, ideally a few hundred" and scale pixel ratio under load ([R3F scaling](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/blob/master/docs/advanced/scaling-performance.mdx)) [V], and MDN requires honouring `prefers-reduced-motion` because "certain kinds of motion can be a trigger for vestibular disorders, epilepsy, and migraine" ([MDN](https://github.com/mdn/content/blob/main/files/en-us/web/css/reference/at-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion/index.md)) [V]; the hero should ship a static poster as the LCP candidate and mount the canvas after LCP in a fixed-aspect box [I].

## The CCO has 2026 budget, and the trust pack decides the deal

The only survey-grade "why now" the notes could retrieve points at the compliance function. In the IAA, ACA Group and Yuter Compliance survey of **411 SEC-registered adviser firms** (fieldwork late April to May 2026), **85%** named AI the top compliance topic for 2026, a 28-point rise on 2025 and the most dominant single response in the survey's 21-year history; **72%** increased compliance testing around AI, 80% have formally adopted AI tools, and 86% keep an inventory of AI tools ([ACA Group](https://www.acaglobal.com/news-and-announcements/survey-ai-dominates-compliance-priorities-at-historic-margin-as-firms-move-from-awareness-to-action/)) [V]. Deloitte's 2026 investment management outlook expects AI to be managed by "a central team with enterprisewide authority" that prioritises a model inventory, model risk assessments, "clear data lineage", vendor vetting, "defined human-in-the-loop thresholds, audit trails and incident-response protocols" ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/financial-services-industry-outlooks/investment-management-industry-outlook.html)) [V]. That artefact list is the buyer's own vocabulary and the de facto veto body for anything with an LLM in it [I]. Reg S-P's service-provider oversight duty, in force for larger advisers since 3 December 2025 and for smaller ones since 3 June 2026 ([FINRA advisory](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/sec-regulation-s-p-compliance-date-reminder-20251114)) [V], gives Legal a formal reason to veto a vendor that cannot evidence controls [I]. The sponsor most likely to have budget and urgency is therefore the CCO, with the COO as co-sponsor for workflow; the investment CIO and PMs are users whose veto is behavioural ("will this slow me down?") rather than budgetary [I]. No survey giving the share of deals signed by CIO versus COO versus CCO was found [V, absence].

Procurement mechanics are well documented and unforgiving. Regulated-sector sales cycles run **150 to 240 days** with security and compliance review dominating the second half ([ORM](https://orm-tech.com/blog/sales-cycle-length-guide/)) [V]; one fintech selling to enterprise financial institutions averaged 14.6 months with more than four months of technical validation per prospect ([Forth & Scale](https://www.forthandscale.com/case-studies/accelerating-financial-sales)) [V]. Buyers arrive with questionnaires of 200 to 400 questions and may ask for SOC 2 Type II, penetration-test results and data-residency documentation "before procurement will even schedule a call" ([FinTechtris](https://www.fintechtris.com/blog/b2b-fintech-sales-enterprise-financial-institutions); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/b2b-sales-cycle-benchmarks)) [V, attribution approximate]. The AITEC-AIMA Illustrative Questionnaire for vendor technology and cyber security has been adopted by over **250** alternative asset managers and is offered by over **200** vendors ([AITEC DDQ](https://www.aitec.org/ddq)) [V]. Adviser buyers expect "a SOC 2 Type II report with the Security, Confidentiality, and Privacy criteria, an unqualified opinion, and an observation period that ended within the last 12 months" ([Zocks](https://www.zocks.io/blog/soc-2-compliance-for-financial-advisors-what-you-need-to-know)) [V]; most startups obtain Type I first and Type II within 6 to 12 months ([Promise Legal](https://promise.legal/guides/soc2-roadmap)) [V]. EU buyers will require DORA Article 30 clauses in one written document, and for critical or important functions, unrestricted audit and inspection rights, threat-led penetration-testing participation, incident assistance and a tested exit plan; right-to-audit, exit and subcontracting controls are "mandatory clauses you cannot negotiate out", and the ESAs' March 2025 subcontracting amendments permit a veto over adding a US-headquartered sub-processor ([CyAdviso](https://www.cyadviso.com/dora-third-party-ict-risk)) [V]. A decision ledger holding regulatory evidence will usually be classified as supporting a critical or important function, so gnosis should plan for the heavy tier rather than argue for the light one [I]. AI-specific diligence adds three questions: will our data train your models, where is it processed, and which third-party AI services do you use ([TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/ai-supply-chain-risk-vendor-due-diligence/)) [V], plus how non-deterministic outputs are "handled, logged, and audited" ([Ampcus Cyber](https://www.ampcuscyber.com/blogs/third-party-ai-vendor-due-diligence-questionnaire/)) [V]. Gnosis can answer the last question structurally, and that answer belongs on the first page of the DDQ response [I].

### Price by decision volume and seats, not by assets

Three live pricing models exist in the comparable set. Clearwater derives about 95% of revenue from subscriptions "mainly priced in basis points on client AUM", with secondary sources putting typical fees at 0.5 to 2 bps ([MatrixBCG](https://matrixbcg.com/blogs/how-it-works/clearwateranalytics)) [V, analyst blog, not a filing]. Enfusion's S-1 showed net dollar retention of **122.4%** at 30 June 2021 with 98% recurring revenue ([Enfusion S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1868912/000155837021012603/tmb-20210917xs1.htm)) [V]. Bloomberg's terminal is reported at **USD 31,980** per seat per year for 2026 ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/financial-data-terminals/bloomberg-terminal/)) [V, aggregator]; AlphaSense is estimated at USD 15,000 to 20,000 per seat ([SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/alphasense-pricing)) [V, third-party]; Essentia Enterprise is priced "based on the number of portfolios analyzed" [V]; Bipsync, Alpha Theory, Verity, Backstop, Dynamo and the OMS vendors publish nothing [V, absence]. AUM basis-point pricing works for Clearwater because its work scales with assets; a decision ledger's cost-to-serve scales with decision volume, decision types and approver seats, so bps pricing would be hard to defend to a CFO, would under-monetise high-velocity hedge funds, and would invite comparison to the alpha-uplift claims gnosis chooses not to make [I]. The preferred structure is an annual platform fee (event store, policy engine, audit log, MCP server) plus per-decision-type modules plus a per-named-approver seat band, with LLM-dependent features metered or capped separately so InfoSec can switch them off without touching the ledger price [I]. Gartner forecasts AI-governance platform spend of **USD 492m in 2026**, passing USD 1bn by 2030 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-17-gartner-global-ai-regulations-fuel-billion-dollar-market-for-ai-governance-platforms)) [V, cross-industry], which sizes the budget line the CCO wedge draws on. Since every direct peer is quote-only, publishing an indicative price band could itself differentiate a startup with no references [I].

### Guideline exceptions are a crowded wedge; AI-governance lineage is open

The obvious wedge is the wrong one. Guideline-exception management already has entrenched tooling inside the OMS and compliance suites: Limina supports "managing breaches and requesting overrides without using separate compliance applications" ([Limina](https://www.limina.com/solutions/pre-post-trade-investment-compliance)) [V]; Funds-Axis, Linedata and Allvue bundle exception workflow with monitoring ([Funds-Axis](https://funds-axis.com/galaxy/investment-compliance-monitoring-software/); [Linedata](https://www.linedata.com/asset-managers/pre-and-post-trade-investment-compliance-software-tailored-asset-managers-mancos)) [V]; and EY built its own ML tool, SARGE, reporting 30 to 45% cost reductions from exception-management projects ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/wealth-asset-management/how-automation-is-transforming-compliance-in-wealth)) [V]. The evidence-backed open lane is AI-governance decision lineage (the CCO's 85% priority plus Deloitte's artefact list, with no incumbent tool found) and investment-committee decision capture, where Deloitte reports a private-markets division that "compressed preparation time for investment committee memos from two weeks to two days" ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/financial-services-industry-outlooks/investment-management-industry-outlook.html)) [V] and Brownloop claims a 70% cut in IC-memo preparation at a USD 100bn PE firm ([Brownloop](https://www.brownloop.com/case-study/revolutionizing-investment-committee-visibility-with-brownloop-cloud-and-kairos-ai/)) [V, single vendor case]. Guideline exceptions should be played as the approval record for decisions the OMS never sees (mandate changes, IPS deviations, multi-fund allocations), never as a pre-trade engine [I]. Corporate-action elections, model-change sign-off, valuation-committee and proxy records are plausible later wedges with no retrieved evidence on pain or tooling [V, absence].

Messaging should borrow the buyer's vocabulary: "model inventory", "data lineage", "audit trail", "human-in-the-loop threshold", "acceptable use policy", "compliance testing", "explain why specific decisions were made", "zero retention" [V, from Deloitte, ACA and DDQ sources]. Describing gnosis as "operating infrastructure for decisions" aligns with Deloitte's call to "engineer AI as an operating infrastructure rather than a collection of productivity tools" ([Deloitte State of AI 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html)) [V]. Phrases likely to trigger objections, inferred from what DDQs probe rather than from a sourced objection study, are "the AI recommends", "autonomous", "learns from your decisions" (unless immediately qualified by the hard gate), "black box", "replaces analysts" and "no integration needed" [I]. Against "we already have Aladdin or Charles River", concede pre-trade compliance and order workflow to the OMS and position gnosis as the record of the decisions that precede and surround orders plus the lineage layer for AI-assisted decisions, a category the OMS vendors' self-descriptions do not claim [I]. Against "we do this in Excel and Outlook", the lever is regulatory: Reg S-P oversight, DORA audit access and the 72% rise in AI compliance testing create record-keeping demands that email cannot meet, and the bitemporal replay demo is the proof [I].

With no reference customers, the proof strategy is a designed pilot plus discounted design partnerships. Practitioner guidance is that "companies should never wait for a technical pilot to conclude before starting the business case or legal review" and that leading with a pre-packaged "Trust Pack" pre-empts the hurdles that stall deals ([FinTechtris](https://www.fintechtris.com/blog/b2b-fintech-sales-enterprise-financial-institutions); [Forth & Scale](https://www.forthandscale.com/case-studies/accelerating-financial-sales)) [V, attribution approximate]. A credible 60-to-90-day pilot scopes one decision type across two or three funds and PMs with one CCO approver, runs on read-only feeds so InfoSec classifies it as low-risk, and states success in the buyer's own terms: 100% of in-scope decisions sealed with a policy check and approval chain, every agent tool call in lineage, an as-of reconstruction for a past date the compliance team chooses, and time-to-record measured against the email baseline [I]. Design partners should be two or three SEC-registered advisers whose CCO has AI governance as a 2026 objective, priced at a discount in exchange for a named reference, a co-authored exam-readiness case study and participation in the AITEC-AIMA DDQ programme [I]. ## Conclusion

The evidence reframes what gnosis is selling. To a regulator the product is the tuple of intent, timing and approver that Western Asset, Two Sigma, Macquarie and Woodford lacked; to a portfolio manager it is the first feedback loop on the sell decision, the one place peer-reviewed research shows institutions reliably destroy value; to a buyer with no references to call, the product's credibility rests on claims that can be verified inside a 60-day pilot without trusting the vendor. That last point is the strategic insight. Every competitor's headline number (150 bps, 93% win rate, "weeks to seconds") requires the buyer to believe the vendor; every one of gnosis's strongest claims (100% of decisions sealed by construction, byte-identical replay, sub-hour retrieval, citations that can only point at ledger objects) can be tested by the buyer's own compliance team on their own past date. A startup without references should sell only claims of the second kind.

The window is defined by regulation rather than technology. Chat over the firm's own notes has been commoditised since November 2024, research-management systems have been absorbed into terminals and exchanges, and the OMS vendors will eventually add decision objects with approval steps. What none of them has, and what the 2026 SEC exam cycle, FINRA's first statement on AI agents, the FCA's end-of-2026 SM&CR guidance and DORA's registers now demand, is a replayable, gated, attributable record of the decisions that precede and surround orders. Gnosis's design already answers that demand; the remaining work is to make the record legally complete (Article 74 fields, versioned policy, order linkage), attested rather than asserted (SOC 2 processing integrity, hash chain), and packaged so a CCO can buy it in one budget cycle.
