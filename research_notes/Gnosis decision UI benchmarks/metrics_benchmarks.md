## Key Question 1 — Decision-quality measurement science and competitor outcome claims (hit rate, payoff ratio, Brier/calibration, Essentia, Alpha Theory, Inalytics, Cabot, "Selling Fast and Buying Slow")

### Takeaway
The industry's decision-quality vocabulary is already standardised around two numbers — hit rate (share of decisions that added value) and payoff / win-loss ratio (average gain on good decisions ÷ average loss on bad ones) — decomposed by decision type (pick, entry timing, sizing, scale-in, size-adjust, scale-out, exit). Competitors publish outcome claims in the 94–180 bps/yr range (Essentia, Cabot) and +3.9–4.3% annualised (Alpha Theory), all vendor-reported on self-selected client samples with no disclosed n; the peer-reviewed evidence (Inalytics database, Akepanidtaworn et al. 2023) says the average manager's hit rate is ~50%, skill sits in buys, and sells underperform even random selling. gnosis should not compete on bps-of-alpha claims it cannot audit; it should publish the same decomposed hit-rate/payoff metrics computed from its sealed ledger, add calibration (Brier) for stated probabilities, and make the sell-side gap its headline decision-quality story.

### Cited Findings

**Metric definitions in use by the market**
- Essentia's Behavioral Alpha Score is derived from daily equity and ETF holdings data and "measures investor decision-making skill"; it reflects seven decision types: stock picking, entry timing, sizing, scaling in, size adjusting, scaling out and exit timing **[V]** — [Essentia, The Behavioral Alpha Benchmark (product page)](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/product/behavioral-alpha-benchmark/); [Essentia, Introducing the Behavioral Alpha Benchmark (blog)](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-blog/)
- Each PM's score is calculated from the overall decision **hit rate** ("percentage of decisions that added value") and **decision payoff ratio** ("ratio of value added by the average good decision to the value destroyed by the average bad decision") **[V]** — [Essentia, Behavioral Alpha Benchmark white paper page](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-paper/)
- Inalytics frames the two questions as: does a manager get more decisions right than wrong (hit rate), and do the good decisions offset the poor ones (win/loss ratio) **[V]** — [The Hedge Fund Journal, "Identifying Manager Skill"](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/identifying-manager-skill/)
- Academic support for preferring win-loss ratio over hit rate: a 2015 paper in *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management* argues the win–loss ratio is "an ability signal of mutual fund managers ... less influenced by luck" **[V, title-level only]** — [Springer, Financial Markets and Portfolio Management (2015)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-015-0255-3)

**Base rates for hit rate and payoff (what a PM population actually looks like)**
- Average hit rate across portfolios in the Inalytics database: **49.6%** — "managers get slightly less than half of all their decisions right", overturning the folk assumption of 6-in-10 **[V; n not disclosed in snippet; database described as $22 trillion of trades over 20 years]** — [WealthBriefingAsia, "Fund Managers Wrong More Often Than Not"](https://www.wealthbriefingasia.com/article.php/Fund-Managers-Wrong-More-Often-Than-Not-_dash_-Research); [Hedgeweek, Inalytics skills-based data model](https://www.hedgeweek.com/inalytics-launches-skills-based-data-model-manager-selection/)
- Average win/loss ratio in the Inalytics database: **102%** ("alpha from good decisions is 2% more than that lost from poorer ones") **[V]** — [WealthBriefingAsia](https://www.wealthbriefingasia.com/article.php/Fund-Managers-Wrong-More-Often-Than-Not-_dash_-Research)
- Predictive power: a higher win-loss ratio in one year is associated with **2–4% additional risk-adjusted return** in the next year **[V; from Inalytics/GLG analysis, n not disclosed]** — [Hedgeweek, "Inalytics and GLG examine what makes a manager skilful"](https://www.hedgeweek.com/inalytics-and-glg-examine-what-makes-manager-skilful/); [IPE, Inalytics manager-selection tool](https://www.ipe.com/inalytics-launches-manager-selection-analysis-tool-with-skills-based-data/10061188.article)
- Essentia Behavioral Alpha Benchmark, Q3 2024 ranking: for the fifth straight quarter a majority of PMs added value vs benchmark; **48% of mutual funds** had Behavioral Alpha Scores > 50 (i.e., net value added through the tracked decision skills) vs **33% of active ETFs** **[V; fund counts not in snippet]** — [Essentia, Behavioral Alpha Benchmark Q3 2024](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-q3-2024/); earlier ranking: [Q1 2024](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-q1-2024/)

### Inferences
- The only vendor whose methodology is public enough for gnosis to replicate is Essentia (hit rate × payoff ratio over seven decision types). Adopting the identical decomposition is a low-cost way to be benchmarkable by the same allocators who read the Behavioral Alpha Benchmark rankings [I].
- All three uplift claims (Essentia 150 bps, Cabot >150 bps, Alpha Theory +3.9%/yr) share the same evidentiary weakness: undisclosed n, client self-selection, and (for Alpha Theory) a frictionless counterfactual. A neutral evaluator would discount them heavily; gnosis gains more from a pre-registered, smaller-magnitude claim than from matching them [I].
- Because hit rates cluster at ~50% and payoff ratios near 100%, small measured improvements are within noise for a single fund over a year; publishing confidence intervals (or requiring ≥ 8 quarters) is necessary for any outcome claim to survive due diligence [I].
- The JoF 2023 result implies that a platform that forces a documented, precedent-linked rationale at exit (rather than entry) attacks the largest documented, fixable performance leak; this is the natural "beat" story versus tools that focus on sizing (Alpha Theory) or on retrospective analytics (Essentia/Cabot) [I].

### Gaps
- Exact magnitudes from "Selling Fast and Buying Slow" (number of portfolios, trades, AUM, date range, and the bps/yr by which sells underperform random) were not captured: all primary hosts (Wiley, NBER, SSRN) were egress-blocked. Unverified-this-session recollection: ~780 portfolios, several million trades, 2000–2016, sells underperforming a random counterfactual by tens-to-~100 bps/yr — treat as unverified.
- Essentia's sample sizes (number of PMs/funds in each quarterly ranking; number of clients behind the 150 bps claim) — no public figure found in snippets; the white-paper page was blocked.
- Inalytics: exact publication dates and n for the 49.6% / 102% figures not captured (articles were blocked); the database description ($22trn, 20 years) came from a 2016-era launch article.
- Cabot: no method disclosure found for the ">150 bps" claim; no post-acquisition FactSet figures found.
- No academic base-rate for Brier scores of buy-side PMs on investment outcomes was found (GJP figures are geopolitical questions; the central-bank figure is the closest financial analogue).
- "Resulting" (Annie Duke's term) and base-rate discipline: no quantitative benchmark exists in the sources found.

## Key Question 2 — Operational and process metrics (documented rationale, proposal-to-approval time, exception turnaround, pre-trade compliance false positives, regulatory-request retrieval, corporate-action errors, documentation time)

### Takeaway
Hard public numbers exist only at the edges: SEC examiners give advisers roughly 10–14 days to start producing documents and routine exams run 60–120 days; AML-style monitoring systems run 90–97% false-positive alert rates at $25–50 of analyst cost per alert; DTCC puts the industry cost of corporate actions at $58bn. For the metrics closest to gnosis's core (share of decisions with a documented rationale, proposal-to-approval cycle time, guideline-exception turnaround, PM/analyst time on documentation) no public figure was found — which is itself a positioning opportunity: gnosis can define and publish the reference numbers.

## Key Question 3 — AI-assistance quality (recommendation acceptance/override, hallucination and unsupported-claim rates, citation accuracy, precedent-retrieval precision/recall, tool-selection accuracy)

### Takeaway
The regulated-domain reference points are stark: clinicians override 46–96% of CDS alerts (90% for drug-interaction alerts), and even citation-grounded legal RAG tools hallucinated in 17% (Lexis+ AI) to 33% (Westlaw AI-AR) of pre-registered test queries versus 58–80% for general chatbots. Grounded-summarisation hallucination rates for the best models are now 0.6–3.3% depending on dataset, while frontier reasoning models still exceed 10% on Vectara's harder 2025 set. gnosis's structural advantages — a closed six-verb outcome space, read-only tools over a sealed ledger, and citations that can only point at ledger objects — let it target unsupported-claim and broken-citation rates near zero, and publish acceptance/override rates in a band that proves the recommendations are neither ignored nor rubber-stamped.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford RegLab / HAI "Hallucination-Free? ## Key Question 4 — Platform reliability, performance, and determinism (uptime SLAs, RTO/RPO under DORA and FCA, p95 latency, incumbent SLAs, reproducibility standards)

### Takeaway
The regulatory frame is fixed: DORA has applied since 17 Jan 2025 and the FCA's PS21/3 operational-resilience regime has been fully in force since 31 Mar 2025, both requiring firms to set impact tolerances (and, under DORA, RTO/RPO) per important business service / critical function — but neither prescribes numeric RTO/RPO, and no incumbent (Aladdin, Charles River, Enfusion) publishes an uptime SLA in an accessible source. gnosis should therefore set contractual targets that are defensible to a DORA/FCA third-party-risk reviewer (99.9% floor, 99.95% for ledger reads, RTO ≤ 4h / RPO ≤ 15 min) and, uniquely, publish deterministic-replay results, which no incumbent advertises.
