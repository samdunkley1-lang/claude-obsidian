# Midland Global Technology Fund: Derivatives Usage Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

**To:** Portfolio Manager, Midland Global Technology Fund; Investment Team; Trading Desk; Risk; Compliance
**From:** Chief Investment Officer and Head of Risk, on behalf of the Board of the Manager
**Date:** 2025-01-01
**Subject:** Derivatives Usage Policy for the Fund: permitted purposes, counterparty standards and binding exposure limits

### 1. Why this policy exists

The Midland Global Technology Fund (the "Fund") is permitted by its prospectus and its Investment Policy Statement to use derivatives for hedging and for efficient portfolio management. Derivatives introduce leverage, counterparty and operational risks that do not arise from holding listed equities directly. This memorandum sets out the conditions under which the Fund may use them. It binds the portfolio manager, the trading desk and every delegate acting for the Fund, and it should be read alongside the Counterparty and Broker Policy and the Investment Guidelines and Restrictions.

### 2. Permitted purposes and instruments

Derivatives may be used only to hedge an existing or anticipated exposure, to gain or reduce market exposure more efficiently than by trading the underlying securities, or to manage currency risk. They may not be used to create economic leverage that would take the Fund's market exposure beyond what a fully invested equity portfolio would carry. Permitted instruments are exchange-traded futures and options on equity indices, single-stock options, forward foreign exchange contracts and equity swaps. Any other instrument requires the prior written agreement of the Chief Investment Officer and Risk before it may be traded.

### 3. Binding limits

The following limits are mandatory. Each is measured against the net asset value of the Fund as most recently determined by the administrator, after giving effect to the proposed transaction. Risk tests each limit daily and the order management system blocks any transaction that would cause a limit to be exceeded.

Gross notional. Gross notional exposure through derivatives shall not exceed 20% of net asset value. Gross notional is the sum of the absolute notional values of all open derivative positions, without netting.

Counterparty exposure. Mark-to-market exposure to any single derivatives counterparty shall not exceed 15% of net asset value. Exposure is measured net of collateral received under an enforceable agreement.

Counterparty quality. Derivatives may only be transacted with counterparties rated A- or better by at least one recognised rating agency. Where a derivatives counterparty's rating is withdrawn or falls below that level, no new transactions may be entered into with it, and Risk shall determine with the portfolio manager how existing positions are to be run off or novated.

Uncollateralised exposure. Uncollateralised derivatives exposure shall not exceed 5% of net asset value. Exposure is uncollateralised to the extent that it is not covered by collateral actually received and held.

### 4. Context: how much the Fund has actually used derivatives

For background, and without creating any limit or expectation for the future, Risk notes that over recent years gross notional derivatives exposure has typically been around 8% of net asset value. The figure is not monitored as a threshold and the portfolio manager may operate above or below it within the limits in section 3.

### 5. Breaches and exceptions

If a limit in section 3 is exceeded without a trade, for example through market movement in the underlying or a change in net asset value, the portfolio manager must notify Risk and Compliance at once, agree a remediation plan and restore compliance as soon as is consistent with shareholders' interests.

The portfolio manager may request a temporary exception to any limit in section 3 by submitting a written rationale. Approval requires the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk acting together, and no person may approve a request that they themselves submitted. An exception is granted for a defined period not exceeding 30 days and lapses automatically at the end of that period unless renewed through the same process.

### 6. Operational requirements

Every over-the-counter derivative must be documented under a master agreement approved by Legal, with a credit support annex where collateral is to be exchanged. Positions are valued daily by the administrator using independent sources, and Risk reconciles counterparty exposure and collateral balances on each business day.

### 7. Review and records

This policy is reviewed by the board annually and whenever the Fund's use of derivatives, its counterparties or the regulatory environment changes materially. Risk keeps the daily monitoring output, breach notifications and exception approvals in accordance with the Manager's record retention schedule and makes them available to the board and the auditors on request.
