# Midland Global Technology Fund: Liquidity Risk Management Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

This Liquidity Risk Management Policy (the "Policy") is adopted by the board of the management company (the "Manager") of the Midland Global Technology Fund (the "Fund"). Its purpose is to ensure that the Fund can meet redemption requests in the ordinary course without disadvantaging remaining shareholders, and that the liquidity profile of the portfolio remains consistent with the dealing terms offered in the prospectus. The Policy binds the portfolio manager, the investment team, Risk and the administrator, and it applies to every asset held by or on behalf of the Fund.

For the purposes of this Policy, net asset value means the value most recently determined by the administrator. A holding is classified as illiquid where, under the Manager's liquidity classification standard, it cannot be sold at or close to its carrying value within the period assumed for the Fund's dealing cycle under normal market conditions; unlisted securities are classified as illiquid unless Risk determines otherwise. Average daily volume means the average traded volume of a security over the trailing period used in the Manager's liquidity model, and normal market conditions are conditions in which the relevant markets are open and functioning without exceptional disruption.

## Portfolio liquidity limits

The board has fixed a ceiling on the portion of the Fund that may be held in assets that cannot readily be sold. Holdings classified as illiquid shall not exceed 10% of net asset value. This limit is tested by Risk on every dealing day, and the order management system blocks any purchase that would cause it to be exceeded.

The complementary requirement is a floor on readily realisable assets. At least 80% of net asset value shall be realisable within five business days under normal market conditions. Realisability is assessed by the Manager's liquidity model, which applies conservative participation assumptions to each holding and aggregates the result across the portfolio.

For context, and without creating any limit, Risk notes that at the last review illiquid holdings represented approximately 3% of net asset value. That observation describes the portfolio as it stood on the review date; it is not a threshold and is not monitored as one.

## Position-level liquidity

Liquidity is also monitored at the level of the individual holding, using a days to liquidate measure. No single position shall require more than 5 business days to liquidate at 25% of average daily volume. The days to liquidate figure for each position is produced by the liquidity model on every dealing day, and a position that exceeds the limit is reported to the portfolio manager and to Risk for a review of whether it should be reduced.

## Swing pricing

To protect continuing shareholders from the dilutive effect of dealing costs generated by large flows, the Fund operates a swing pricing mechanism. Swing pricing shall be applied on any dealing day on which net flows exceed 2% of net asset value. On such a day the administrator adjusts the dealing price by the swing factor determined under the Manager's swing pricing procedure, which reflects the estimated cost of trading the portfolio to meet the net flow. The swing factor itself is set and reviewed by the Valuation Committee and is not published in advance.

## Stress testing and escalation

Risk conducts liquidity stress testing on the portfolio, applying redemption scenarios and reduced market volume assumptions, and reports the results to the board. Where stress results indicate that the Fund could not meet a plausible redemption scenario within its dealing terms, Risk escalates to the Chief Investment Officer and the board, and the portfolio manager is required to propose actions to improve the liquidity profile.

## Breaches and exceptions

If a limit in this Policy is exceeded otherwise than through a trade, for example because of a change in a security's classification, a fall in market volumes or a large redemption, the portfolio manager must notify Risk and Compliance without delay, agree a remediation plan and restore compliance as soon as is consistent with shareholders' interests.

The portfolio manager may request a temporary exception to any limit in this Policy by submitting a written rationale. Approval requires the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk acting together, and no person may approve a request that they themselves submitted. An exception is granted for a defined period not exceeding 30 days and lapses automatically at the end of that period unless renewed through the same process.

## Review and records

The board reviews this Policy annually and whenever the Fund's dealing terms, its investment strategy or the regulatory environment change materially. Risk retains the daily liquidity model output, stress test results, breach notifications and exception approvals in accordance with the Manager's record retention schedule and makes them available to the board and the auditors on request.
