# Midland Global Technology Fund: Investment Guidelines and Restrictions

Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

## About this document

The board of the Manager has approved the following rules for building and maintaining the portfolio of the Midland Global Technology Fund. They sit underneath the Investment Policy Statement and go into more detail on how concentrated the Fund is allowed to become in any name, industry, country or sleeve, and on the securities it may never buy.

- Everyone who makes or executes investment decisions for the Fund is bound by these rules.
- They cover every asset the Fund holds, however acquired.
- If another Manager policy is stricter on the same point, the stricter rule wins.
- Position sizes are measured against net asset value as most recently struck by the administrator, after the effect of the trade under consideration, with derivative exposure counted on a delta-adjusted basis.

## Position size in a single name

- The weight of any one security may not go above 7% of net asset value.
- A different ceiling applies to a security whose weight in the benchmark is greater than 3%: for such a security the ceiling becomes its benchmark weight plus 2 percentage points.
- Benchmark weights are taken from the index provider's most recent published rebalancing.
- Looking through to the issuer, everything the Fund holds in one issuer (ordinary shares, other share classes, depositary receipts, convertibles and derivative exposure) is added together and may not exceed 10% of net asset value.

## Industry exposure

- The Software sector is capped at 40% of net asset value.
- The Hardware sector is capped at 25% of net asset value.
- Sector assignments are those of the benchmark provider.

## Country exposure

- Issuers domiciled in Taiwan may together represent no more than 15% of net asset value.
- Issuers domiciled in China may together represent no more than 10% of net asset value.
- Domicile, not the place of listing, determines which limit applies.

## Unlisted holdings

- Unlisted companies expected to seek a listing are held in the Private and pre-IPO sleeve.
- Inside the Private and pre-IPO sleeve, a single security may not exceed 5% of net asset value.
- Sleeve holdings also count towards the single security and issuer limits above and are subject to the Liquidity Risk Management Policy.

## Securities the Fund may never buy

- The Firm keeps a Restricted List of issuers about which it or an affiliate holds material non-public information or faces a conflict.
- Buying, or adding to, a security of any issuer on the Firm's Restricted List is forbidden in all circumstances.
- This prohibition is absolute. The Manager cannot waive it, the Chief Investment Officer cannot waive it, Compliance cannot waive it, and no other individual or committee has the power to override it.
- Positions already held when an issuer joins the Restricted List are dealt with only as the Firm directs.

## For information only

- When the Manager opens a new position it is typically sized at approximately 3.5% of net asset value. This is a description of practice, not a rule, and Compliance does not test it.

## When a limit is crossed

- The order management system stops any trade that would push the Fund over any of the limits above.
- If the Fund drifts over a limit without trading, through price moves, flows or corporate actions, the portfolio manager tells Compliance straight away, agrees a plan to fix it, and puts it right as fast as shareholders' interests permit.

## Asking for a temporary exception

- The portfolio manager may ask, in writing and with reasons, for a limited-time exception to any of the concentration limits. The Restricted List prohibition is excluded from this process.
- Approval needs the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance, all of them.
- Whoever made the request cannot also approve it.
- An exception runs for a stated period of at most 30 days and expires automatically unless renewed the same way.

## Upkeep

- The board looks at these rules again every year and whenever the Fund's strategy, benchmark or regulatory setting changes in a material way.
- Compliance files the daily test results, breach reports and exception approvals under the Manager's record retention schedule and produces them for the board or the auditors on request.
