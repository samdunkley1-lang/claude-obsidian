# Midland European Income Fund: Derivatives Usage Policy
Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

## Purpose

- This Policy governs every derivative instrument transacted for the Midland European Income Fund (the "Fund"), whether exchange-traded or over the counter.
- It is approved by the board of the Manager, owned by Risk, and enforced through pre-trade controls in the dealing system together with daily post-trade monitoring.
- Version 2 raises the gross notional ceiling following the board's review of the Fund's currency hedging programme; every other provision is carried forward from version 1 without change of substance.
- Terms defined in the Investment Policy Statement carry the same meaning here.

## Permitted purpose

- Derivatives may be used for hedging purposes only, meaning to reduce a market, currency or interest-rate exposure that already exists in the portfolio.
- Non-hedging derivatives, meaning derivatives that increase market exposure, are prohibited. Synthetic long positions, leverage and directional overlays all fall within this prohibition.
- The portfolio manager must record the exposure being hedged at the time each derivative is entered into.

## Exposure limits

- Gross notional exposure through derivatives shall not exceed 12% of net asset value. Gross notional is the sum of the absolute notional values of all open positions, without netting between contracts.
- Mark-to-market exposure to any single derivatives counterparty shall not exceed 10% of net asset value. Exposure is the positive replacement value of all open contracts with that counterparty, net of collateral received under an enforceable netting arrangement.
- Uncollateralised derivatives exposure shall not exceed 5% of net asset value. Exposure is uncollateralised to the extent that positive replacement value is not covered by collateral the Fund holds.
- Each of these limits is tested before execution as if the trade had already been done, and a trade that would exceed a limit is blocked.

## Counterparty eligibility

- Derivatives may only be transacted with counterparties rated A or better by at least one recognised rating agency.
- A derivatives counterparty downgraded below that level may not be used for new transactions; Risk decides whether existing contracts with it are novated, collateralised further or closed.
- Master agreement and collateral documentation must be in place with every over-the-counter counterparty before the first trade.

## Historical context, for information only

- Over the last three years gross notional derivatives exposure has typically been around 4% of net asset value.
- Usage has consisted mainly of currency forwards hedging non-base-currency dividend income. These bullets describe past practice and create no limit.

## Exceptions

- A temporary exception to any exposure limit, to the counterparty eligibility requirement or to the permitted-purpose restriction may be granted only on a four-eyes basis by the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk.
- Every exception must be documented in writing with its rationale and expiry.
- No exception may remain in force for longer than 30 days.

## Monitoring and breaches

- Risk monitors compliance with this Policy daily.
- Any breach is notified immediately to the portfolio manager and Compliance, entered in the breach register and escalated to the Chief Investment Officer.
- Remediation takes place as soon as practicable, weighing the cost of closing or novating contracts against the interests of unitholders.
- Risk reports derivative exposures, breaches and open exceptions to the board at each scheduled meeting.

## Transition

- Positions compliant with version 1 remain compliant with version 2, since the only change is an increase in the gross notional ceiling.
- Pre-trade control parameters are to be updated on the effective date and the change confirmed to Compliance.

## Records and review

- Confirmations, collateral statements and valuations for every derivative are retained under the Firm's record-keeping procedures.
- The board reviews this Policy annually and whenever the Fund's use of derivatives changes materially.
