# Midland European Income Fund: Investment Guidelines and Restrictions
Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

## Introduction

The board of the Manager has adopted these Guidelines to define the boundaries within which the portfolio of the Midland European Income Fund is constructed. They sit beneath the Investment Policy Statement, which sets the Fund's objective and its cash, turnover, tracking error and currency parameters, and they deal specifically with how concentrated the portfolio may become in any security, issuer, sector or country. The present version reflects the board's decision, taken after its review of concentration risk, to narrow the permitted size of individual holdings; the remaining boundaries carry over from the previous version unchanged.

## How limits are applied

Throughout this document a limit is a maximum weight measured against the Fund's net asset value. The test is applied to the portfolio as it would stand once a contemplated order has been executed, and an order whose execution would push a weight beyond its maximum is not permitted to proceed. Weights are refreshed with each valuation, and the pre-trade control in the dealing system is the primary means of enforcement, with Compliance monitoring the portfolio after trading as a further safeguard.

## Individual holdings

The largest weight the Fund may hold in any single security is 4.5% of net asset value. That maximum gives way where the security in question accounts for more than 3% of the benchmark: in that situation the applicable ceiling becomes the benchmark weight plus 1.5 percentage points, so that the portfolio manager is not forced into a structural underweight in the largest index constituents. Beyond the security level, all instruments issued by the same issuer are aggregated, and that aggregate may not exceed 5.5% of net asset value. Both limits are enforced by blocking any order that would take the portfolio beyond them.

The Small-cap sleeve is subject to a tighter rule of its own. Inside that sleeve, no single security may represent more than 3% of net asset value. This operates cumulatively with the Fund-level maximum: a small-cap holding must satisfy both.

## Sectors and countries

Explicit ceilings apply to the Utilities and Real Estate sectors. The Utilities sector may not account for more than 20% of net asset value, and the Real Estate sector may not account for more than 15% of net asset value. Classification is taken from the benchmark provider.

Ceilings likewise apply to issuers domiciled in Germany and in France. Issuers domiciled in Germany may not, in aggregate, exceed 25% of net asset value, and issuers domiciled in France may not, in aggregate, exceed 25% of net asset value. Domicile is assigned under the Manager's country classification framework, which follows country of incorporation unless a different country of risk has been determined.

## The Restricted List

Separately from any weight-based limit, the Fund may not purchase, or add to, any security of an issuer that appears on the Firm's Restricted List, in any circumstances whatsoever. This is an absolute prohibition. It cannot be waived by the Manager, by the Chief Investment Officer or by Compliance, and the exception procedure described below does not extend to it. When an issuer is placed on the Restricted List, any existing holding is frozen until Compliance directs otherwise.

## A note on ordinary practice

Readers of these Guidelines sometimes ask how large a new holding usually is when it is first established. The Manager's typical initial position size is approximately 2.25% of net asset value. This is offered purely as background: it describes what the portfolio manager tends to do, not what the portfolio manager is required or permitted to do, and departures from it are not breaches.

## Exceptions

Other than the Restricted List prohibition, each limit in these Guidelines may be relaxed temporarily where the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance approve the relaxation on a four-eyes basis. The approval is recorded in writing together with the reason for it and the intended route back to compliance, and it expires no later than 30 days after it is given.

## Breaches

When a limit is breached, whether by a trade or by movements in prices, corporate events or benchmark changes, the portfolio manager informs Compliance without delay. Compliance logs the event, informs the Chief Investment Officer and supervises the return to compliance, which is carried out in an orderly manner that protects unitholders rather than through forced sales. The board receives a report of all breaches and live exceptions at each of its scheduled meetings.

## Review and amendment

The board reviews these Guidelines annually, and earlier if the Investment Policy Statement is amended. Amendments take effect on the date stated beneath the title of this document.
