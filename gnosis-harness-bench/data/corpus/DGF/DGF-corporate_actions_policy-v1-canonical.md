# Midland Diversified Growth Fund: Corporate Actions Election Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

This Policy governs the way in which Midland Asset Management Limited (the "Manager") responds to corporate actions affecting securities held by the Midland Diversified Growth Fund (the "Fund"). Its purpose is to ensure that every election is made on time, in the interests of investors and in a manner that is consistent with the Fund's other investment restrictions. It has been approved by the Board of the Fund and binds the Manager, the operations team and the custodian's instructing agents.

A corporate action is any event initiated by an issuer or a third party that requires or permits the Fund to make a choice, including scrip dividend alternatives, rights issues, tender offers, exchange offers, schemes of arrangement and consent solicitations. The custodian notifies the Manager of each event and its deadline through the corporate actions platform, and the operations team records every notification, the default that applies to it, any departure from the default and the instruction ultimately given.

The Policy works by fixing a default for each common type of election. The default applies automatically unless the portfolio manager decides otherwise and documents the reasons, and in some cases the decision to depart from the default requires more senior approval. A default is a binding rule: an instruction that departs from it without the required documentation or approval is a breach of this Policy.

Scrip dividend alternatives shall be taken in cash by default. A scrip election requires a documented rationale from the portfolio manager explaining why receiving shares serves investors better than receiving cash. A scrip election is prohibited altogether where the post-election position would exceed the single security limit in the Investment Guidelines, and the operations team must confirm the projected position against that limit before any scrip instruction is released. For context, and without altering the rule, the Board notes that in recent years the majority of scrip alternatives offered to the Fund have been taken in cash.

Rights issues shall be taken up by default. Where the portfolio manager considers that the Fund should instead allow its rights to lapse, or should sell the nil-paid rights in the market, that decision requires a documented rationale from the portfolio manager which must be recorded before the instruction is given. The rationale should address the terms of the issue, the effect on the Fund's holding and any concentration consequences of taking up the rights in full.

Tender offers shall be declined by default. Tendering any part of a holding requires approval of the Chief Investment Officer, given in writing after considering the portfolio manager's recommendation, the offer terms and the likely outcome for holders who do not tender. A partial tender is treated in the same way as a full tender for this purpose.

The election deadline rule applies to every event regardless of the default. Elections shall be instructed to the custodian no later than 2 business days before the custodian deadline. The operations team is responsible for tracking the custodian deadline for each event, for obtaining the portfolio manager's decision in good time, and for confirming that the custodian has acknowledged the instruction. An instruction sent later than this internal deadline is a breach of this Policy even if the custodian accepts it.

The Manager may seek an exception to any of the defaults or to the election deadline rule where the circumstances of a particular event make the default inappropriate and the standard departure process cannot be followed. An exception requires the joint written approval of the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance, must record the reasons for it, and lapses 30 days after approval unless renewed through the same process. No exception may be granted after the event to regularise an instruction that has already been given in breach of this Policy.

Compliance reviews the corporate actions log on each business day for events approaching their custodian deadline without an instruction, for departures from the default without the required documentation, and for instructions given late. Any such finding is reported to the portfolio manager and the Chief Investment Officer on the day it is identified and recorded in the breach log; where the Fund has suffered a loss the matter is escalated to the Board together with the remedial action taken. Records of notifications, decisions, approvals and instructions are retained in accordance with the Manager's record keeping policy.

The Board reviews this Policy periodically and whenever the custodian's processing arrangements change materially. Amendments take effect on the date stated by the Board and supersede all earlier versions.
