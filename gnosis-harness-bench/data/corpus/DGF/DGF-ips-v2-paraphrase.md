# Midland Diversified Growth Fund: Investment Policy Statement

Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

The Board of the Midland Diversified Growth Fund has adopted this Statement to define the mandate it gives to Midland Asset Management Limited as investment manager. The document describes what the Fund is trying to achieve, the instruments it may use and, most importantly, the boundaries within which the Manager's discretion operates. Those boundaries are obligations rather than aspirations. Whenever a percentage appears in this Statement it should be read as a proportion of the Fund's net asset value, calculated as it would stand once a contemplated transaction had settled.

The Fund's purpose is the growth of capital over the long term. It pursues that purpose by blending global equities, sovereign and corporate bonds and cash in proportions that the Manager varies according to its assessment of value and risk. The Board judges the outcome against the composite benchmark named in the Prospectus, and it expects the Manager to take meaningful positions away from that benchmark while remaining inside the limits below.

Liquidity is the first discipline. The Manager must keep cash and cash equivalents at 2 per cent of net asset value or more at every point in time, so that redemptions and margin calls can be met without forced sales. At the other end, the Manager may not allow cash and cash equivalents to rise above 25 per cent of net asset value, since a fund that is substantially uninvested is not delivering its mandate. In ordinary conditions the Manager has told the Board that it expects cash to settle at around 3 per cent of net asset value; the Board records that expectation here as context and not as a rule.

Breadth is the second discipline. The portfolio must at all times comprise no fewer than 60 distinct positions. A count that falls short of this number does not halt dealing, but it does oblige the Manager to issue a warning to Compliance and to refrain from any trade that would reduce the count further until the shortfall is cured. In a similar vein, annual portfolio turnover may not exceed 200 per cent of average net assets; an excess is treated as a warning to be explained to the Board rather than as a blocked transaction. For perspective, the Fund's turnover has historically averaged approximately 120 per cent per annum, a figure that describes past behaviour and carries no normative weight.

Active risk is the third discipline. Ex-ante tracking error relative to the benchmark must not exceed 600 bps. Because tracking error is estimated from a model rather than observed, the Manager must use the risk system approved by Risk for the purpose, and any proposed trade whose modelled effect would carry the estimate beyond that ceiling is prohibited.

The Board also constrains where the Fund's capital may concentrate. Holdings in the Technology sector may not together exceed 20 per cent of net asset value. Holdings in issuers domiciled in United States may not together exceed 50 per cent of net asset value. Currency exposure that is not hedged back to the base currency may not exceed 30 per cent of net asset value, whether that exposure arises from securities, cash balances or derivative overlays.

Within the fixed income allocation the Manager must keep the portfolio's modified duration between 2 and 7 years, measured across all interest rate sensitive instruments including derivatives. Holdings rated below investment grade may not exceed 12 per cent of net asset value. The Manager is further prohibited from purchasing any security rated below B or its equivalent; a downgrade of an existing holding below that level does not force a sale but does prevent any addition to the position.

Where the Manager believes that a departure from one of these obligations is in investors' interests, it may seek an exception. For the cash floor and ceiling, the position count, turnover, the Technology and United States limits, the below investment grade ceiling and the minimum purchase rating, an exception is valid only when the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance have all approved it. For tracking error, unhedged currency and duration, the approving parties are the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk. An approved exception ceases to have effect 30 days after it is granted unless it is renewed in the same way, and the Manager must document the grounds for each request.

Compliance tests every obligation in this Statement against the official valuation on each business day and reports any excess to the portfolio manager and the Chief Investment Officer without delay. Breaches that arise from market movement are corrected in an orderly manner; breaches that arise from dealing are escalated to the Board with an explanation of cause and remedy. The Board revisits this Statement periodically and whenever the Fund's objective, benchmark or investor base changes materially, and no earlier version retains any force once a successor has taken effect.
