# Midland Diversified Growth Fund: Investment Guidelines and Restrictions

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

## Introduction

Midland Asset Management Limited manages the Midland Diversified Growth Fund under a mandate whose broad shape is set by the Investment Policy Statement. This companion document narrows that mandate. It lists the concentration ceilings and the absolute prohibition that apply to every trade, and it explains how the Manager, Compliance and the Board deal with an excess. Nothing here loosens a restriction found elsewhere; if two documents pull in different directions, the tighter one wins.

Percentages throughout are proportions of the Fund's net asset value, evaluated as the portfolio would stand after the trade under consideration had been completed. Compliance carries out that evaluation before execution through the order management system and again on each business day against the official valuation.

## Concentration ceilings

The table below lists the ceilings that the Manager may not cross without a previously approved exception. Each is a firm obligation, expressed as the largest permitted proportion of net asset value.

| Item | What is measured | Ceiling |
| - | - | - |
| A | Any one security, measured across the whole Fund | 4% |
| B | Everything issued by any one issuer, including entities under its common control | 5% |
| C | Any one security held within the Alternatives sleeve | 10% |
| D | The Energy sector in aggregate | 15% |
| E | The Healthcare sector in aggregate | 20% |
| F | Issuers domiciled in Japan in aggregate | 20% |
| G | Issuers domiciled in United Kingdom in aggregate | 20% |

Item C operates alongside item A rather than replacing it: a security in the Alternatives sleeve that would count towards both is tested against both. Sector membership follows the classification standard used in the Manager's portfolio system, and domicile follows the country of risk assigned by the Manager's data vendor, or the country of incorporation where no country of risk is available.

## Absolute prohibition

| Item | Prohibition | Can it be waived? |
| - | - | - |
| H | No security of any issuer on the Firm's Restricted List may be bought, nor may an existing holding in such an issuer be increased, in any circumstances | No. Neither the Manager, nor the Chief Investment Officer, nor Compliance, nor any other person or committee has authority to waive this restriction |

Compliance keeps the Restricted List current and informs the Manager immediately when an issuer is added or removed. A holding acquired before its issuer joined the list may be kept or sold, but not added to, while the issuer remains listed. A purchase made in breach of item H is reversed at the Manager's cost and reported to the Board.

## Descriptive note

The Board records, for context only, that the Manager's typical initial position size is approximately 2% of net asset value. That figure reflects how the Manager tends to build positions; it is neither a floor nor a ceiling and is not tested by Compliance.

## Exceptions

An exception to any of items A to G is valid only if the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance have each approved it in writing before the relevant trade. The request must set out why the departure serves investors and how the portfolio will return within the ceiling. Approval lapses 30 days after it is given unless renewed in the same way.

No exception of any kind is available for item H.

## Handling an excess

When a ceiling is crossed because prices have moved rather than because of a trade, the Manager reports the position to the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance as soon as it is identified and brings the portfolio back inside the ceiling in an orderly way that does not disadvantage investors. When a ceiling is crossed because of a trade, the event is logged as a breach, the trade is examined for root cause, and the Board is informed at its next meeting together with the remedial action taken.

## Review

These Guidelines are revisited by the Board periodically and whenever the Investment Policy Statement changes. A revised version takes effect on the date the Board specifies and replaces all earlier text from that date.
