# Midland Corporate Bond Fund: Valuation and Pricing Policy
Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

This Valuation and Pricing Policy describes how the assets of the Midland Corporate Bond Fund are priced for the purpose of calculating the net asset value, which sources are relied on, what happens when a source fails and which decisions are reserved to the Valuation Committee. It is issued by Midland Asset Management as manager of the Fund and binds the portfolio manager, the fund accounting team, the pricing function and the Valuation Committee itself. The Policy is intended to produce a fair value for every position at every valuation point, applied consistently, so that subscribing and redeeming unitholders are treated equitably.

The starting point is an independent price. Corporate bonds are valued using evaluated prices from the Fund's primary pricing vendor, corroborated against a secondary vendor and, where available, against observable dealer quotations. The pricing function maintains a hierarchy of sources for each instrument type, records which source was used for every position and investigates any material difference between sources before a price is released. Where the primary source does not provide a price, the hierarchy determines which alternative is used, and the reason for the substitution is documented.

Lot size matters for corporate bonds because the price at which a small piece trades can differ materially from the price for an institutional block. For that reason the Policy draws a distinction between round lots and odd lots. Positions held in odd lots shall be valued using odd-lot prices; institutional round-lot prices shall not be applied to odd-lot positions. The pricing function identifies odd lot positions at each valuation point using the market convention for the relevant instrument, and the vendor's odd-lot pricing service, or a dealer quotation for the actual size held, is used for them. Applying a round-lot price to an odd lot is a breach of this Policy whatever the direction of the difference.

Assets for which observable inputs are not available are classified as Level 3 in the fair value hierarchy. Such assets are unusual for this Fund but can arise, for example where a private placement has no vendor coverage or where a distressed issuer's bonds have stopped trading. Any mark applied to a Level 3 asset requires prior approval of the Valuation Committee. The pricing function prepares a valuation memorandum describing the methodology, the inputs and the sensitivity of the mark, and the Committee records its decision and its reasoning before the mark is used in the net asset value.

Prices can also go stale. A price that has not been updated for more than 5 business days is deemed stale and must be referred to the Valuation Committee before use. The pricing function runs a staleness report at each valuation point, and any stale price is escalated with a recommendation as to whether the last price remains representative, whether an alternative source should be used or whether the position should be treated as Level 3 until a reliable price is available. The Committee may confirm the price, substitute another or direct a fair value adjustment, and in each case it documents the basis of its decision.

As a matter of description rather than requirement, the Valuation Committee reviews its pricing sources approximately every 90 days. This review frequency is the Committee's customary practice and is not a rule of the Policy; the Committee may bring a review forward or, where circumstances warrant, allow a longer interval, and neither course is a breach. The review considers the accuracy of each vendor, the number of prices challenged and the outcomes of those challenges.

The Policy permits an exception to the odd lot rule, the Level 3 approval requirement or the stale price procedure only where the portfolio manager and the Valuation Committee both approve it in writing beforehand, with the reason recorded in the Committee's minutes. No other person may grant an exception, and an exception granted after the event does not cure a breach.

Any departure from the Policy that is discovered after a net asset value has been struck is reported to Compliance without delay, its effect on the net asset value is quantified by the fund accounting team and the Firm's error correction procedure determines whether unitholders need to be compensated. The event is recorded in the pricing incident log together with the corrective action.

The Valuation Committee owns this Policy and reviews it periodically and after any material change in the Fund's holdings, its pricing vendors or applicable regulation. The pricing function retains the price source records, staleness reports, valuation memoranda and Committee minutes in line with the Firm's record retention policy.
