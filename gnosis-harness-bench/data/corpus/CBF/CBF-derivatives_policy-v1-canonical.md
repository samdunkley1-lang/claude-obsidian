# Midland Corporate Bond Fund: Derivatives Usage Policy
Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

## Introduction

This Derivatives Usage Policy (the "Policy") governs the use of derivative instruments by the Midland Corporate Bond Fund (the "Fund"). The Fund is a long-only corporate credit portfolio, and derivatives play a supporting role: they allow the portfolio manager to hedge the interest rate, credit spread and currency risks that arise from the bond holdings without disturbing the underlying positions. The Policy is issued by Midland Asset Management (the "Manager"), binds the portfolio manager, the dealing desk and Risk, and operates alongside the Investment Policy Statement, the Investment Guidelines and Restrictions and the Counterparty and Broker Policy.

The operative rules are presented in the tables that follow. Every entry is a binding requirement. Each exposure limit is measured after giving effect to the proposed transaction and must continue to be met at all times thereafter, and percentages are percentages of the Fund's net asset value. The prose between the tables explains how the entries are applied and does not create further requirements.

## Table 1: Permitted use

| Subject | Requirement |
| - | - |
| Purpose | Derivatives may be used for hedging purposes only. |
| Non-hedging derivatives | Derivatives that increase market exposure are prohibited. A derivative is treated as non-hedging where it is not matched to an identified risk in the portfolio that it reduces. |

## Table 2: Exposure limits

| Measure | Limit | Basis of measurement |
| - | - | - |
| Gross notional exposure through derivatives | Shall not exceed 50% of net asset value | Sum of the absolute notional amounts of all open derivative positions |
| Mark-to-market exposure to any single derivatives counterparty | Shall not exceed 10% of net asset value | Net positive replacement value with that counterparty after netting under an enforceable master agreement |
| Uncollateralised derivatives exposure | Shall not exceed 5% of net asset value | Net positive replacement value across all counterparties that is not covered by collateral actually received and held |

## Table 3: Counterparty eligibility

| Subject | Requirement |
| - | - |
| Rating of a derivatives counterparty | Derivatives may only be transacted with counterparties rated A- or better by at least one recognised rating agency. |
| Documentation | Every derivatives counterparty must have executed a master agreement with the Fund, including a collateral annex, before any trade is executed. |

## Applying the tables

The hedging test in Table 1 is applied trade by trade. Before a derivative is dealt, the portfolio manager records the risk it is intended to reduce and the bond positions giving rise to that risk, and Risk confirms that the instrument, its size and its direction are consistent with that purpose. A derivative that would leave the Fund with more market exposure than it had before is not a hedge, whatever it is called, and may not be dealt.

The exposure limits in Table 2 are monitored by Risk at each valuation point using the Fund's risk system. Gross notional is measured without netting long and short positions against each other. Counterparty exposure is measured net of collateral where the master agreement and collateral annex permit netting, and the uncollateralised figure is the residual once collateral held has been deducted.

Where a counterparty ceases to satisfy Table 3, for instance following a downgrade below A-, no new trades may be dealt with it. Existing positions are reviewed with Risk, which decides whether they are to be novated, closed or maintained to maturity under enhanced collateral terms, and the decision is minuted.

## Historical context (not a limit)

Over the last three years gross notional derivatives exposure has typically been around 20% of net asset value. This sentence describes past usage and nothing more. It is not a limit, it is not monitored as one, and gross notional exposure above or below that level is not a breach so long as the limit in Table 2 is observed.

## Exceptions

| Element | Requirement |
| - | - |
| Approvers | The portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk, jointly and in writing |
| Timing | Before the transaction is executed |
| Content | The reason for the exception and the intended route back within the limit |
| Duration | Lapses 30 days after approval unless renewed through the same procedure |

## Breaches and governance

Any breach of this Policy is reported to Compliance without delay and entered in the breach register with its cause and the corrective action. A passive breach, for example one caused by a movement in mark-to-market values or a counterparty downgrade, is corrected under a plan agreed between the portfolio manager and Risk that protects unitholders from forced unwinding. A breach caused by a trade is corrected promptly and escalated to the Chief Investment Officer.

The Fund Oversight Committee reviews this Policy periodically and following any material change in the Fund's use of derivatives, its counterparties or the regulatory framework. Risk retains the hedging records and exposure reports, and Compliance retains the breach register and exception approvals, in accordance with the Firm's record retention policy.
