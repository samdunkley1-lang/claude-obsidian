# Midland Emerging Markets Fund: Counterparty and Broker Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This Counterparty and Broker Policy (the "Policy") governs the selection of, and the Fund's exposure to, the banks, brokers, securities lending agents and other counterparties with which the Midland Emerging Markets Fund (the "Fund") deals.

1.2. The Policy binds the Manager, the portfolio management team, the dealing desk, Operations and any delegate acting for the Fund. It operates alongside the Investment Policy Statement and the Derivatives Usage Policy, and where those documents and this Policy address the same matter the more restrictive provision applies.

## 2. Definitions

2.1. "Exposure" to a securities lending or repo counterparty means the value of securities lent or cash advanced to that counterparty, less the value of collateral received from it under an enforceable arrangement.

2.2. "Annual traded value" means the aggregate value of purchases and sales executed for the Fund over a rolling year.

2.3. "Deposit bank" means any bank with which the Fund places cash on deposit, whether on call or for a fixed term.

2.4. "Affiliated entity" means any entity affiliated with the Manager, including any entity within the Manager's group and any entity in which the Manager or its group holds a controlling interest.

## 3. Counterparty exposure limits

3.1. Exposure to any single securities lending counterparty, and to any single repo counterparty, shall not exceed 15% of net asset value.

3.2. No single executing broker shall account for more than 25% of the Fund's annual traded value; the share of any single executing broker is measured on a rolling basis by the dealing desk and reported to Compliance monthly.

3.3. Each limit in this clause is tested on a post-trade basis, meaning the existing exposure together with the change a proposed transaction would produce. Passive movements in exposure arising from market prices or collateral revaluation do not of themselves constitute a breach, but Operations must restore compliance in an orderly manner once such a movement has been identified.

## 4. Deposit bank eligibility

4.1. Cash deposits may only be placed with a deposit bank rated A- or better.

4.2. Where a deposit bank is downgraded below A- while a deposit is outstanding, no further deposit may be placed with it, and Operations shall withdraw the existing deposit at the earliest date on which it can be withdrawn without penalty.

## 5. Affiliated entities

5.1. No transaction may be entered into with an affiliated entity. This conflict-of-interest prohibition cannot be overridden by any individual or committee, and no exception, waiver or approval process exists in respect of it.

5.2. Compliance maintains the list of affiliated entities and circulates it to the dealing desk and Operations whenever it changes.

## 6. Historical context

6.1. For information, and without creating any limit or expectation, securities lending exposure to the largest counterparty is currently about 7.5% of net asset value. That figure describes the position at the date of this Policy; the binding limit remains that stated in clause 3.

## 7. Approval of counterparties

7.1. Every securities lending counterparty, repo counterparty, executing broker and deposit bank must be approved by Risk and added to the approved counterparty list before the first transaction with it.

7.2. Risk reviews the approved counterparty list at least annually and removes any counterparty that no longer meets the criteria in this Policy.

## 8. Exceptions

8.1. An exception to any limit in clause 3 or clause 4 may be approved only by the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk, acting together.

8.2. A request for an exception must be in writing, must state the rationale and the intended route back to compliance, and must be recorded by Compliance in the exceptions register.

8.3. No exception may remain in force for longer than 30 days.

8.4. No exception is available in respect of clause 5.

## 9. Breach procedure

9.1. Compliance notifies the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk as soon as a breach is identified.

9.2. The portfolio manager, or Operations where the breach is operational in nature, proposes a remediation plan, and Compliance records the breach, its cause and its resolution in the breach log.

9.3. Material breaches, any transaction with an affiliated entity and any breach that remains unresolved once its remediation plan has run its course are reported to the Board.

## 10. Governance, review and records

10.1. This Policy is owned by the Chief Investment Officer and approved by the Board.

10.2. It is reviewed annually and whenever a change to the prospectus, the regulatory status of the Fund or the Manager's group structure affects any provision above.

10.3. Counterparty approvals, exposure reports, rating evidence and exception records are retained in accordance with the Manager's record-keeping policy.
