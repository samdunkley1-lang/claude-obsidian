# Midland Emerging Markets Fund: Investment Guidelines and Restrictions

Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

The Midland Emerging Markets Fund (the "Fund") is managed within a framework of concentration limits, sleeve limits and absolute prohibitions that sit beneath its Investment Policy Statement. This document sets out that framework as it stands in version 2, which the Board has approved and which applies to the Manager, to each member of the portfolio management team and to any delegate appointed for the Fund. If a provision here and a provision of the Investment Policy Statement address the same subject, the stricter of the two governs.

Before turning to the limits themselves, it is worth being clear about how they are applied. Each is assessed on a post-trade footing, so a proposed order is judged by adding its effect to what the Fund already holds and comparing the total with the relevant threshold. Drift caused by prices, corporate events or investor flows is not itself an infringement, although the portfolio manager is expected to bring the portfolio back into line in an orderly way once the drift is noticed. Classification of a holding by sector or country follows the benchmark provider's scheme unless the Valuation Committee has resolved that a different classification is appropriate for that holding.

At the level of the individual holding, the weight of any one security is capped at 5% of net asset value. That cap is displaced where the security carries a weight of more than 4% in the benchmark; for such a security the applicable ceiling is instead the benchmark weight plus 2 percentage points, and it remains the applicable ceiling only for as long as the benchmark condition continues to be met.

Looking through to the issuer, the Fund's combined exposure to any one issuer, taking together every security that issuer has issued, may not be greater than 8% of net asset value. Ordinary shares, preference shares, depositary receipts and any other instrument whose value flows from the issuer are all counted in that aggregation.

The Frontier markets sleeve is subject to its own holding limit. Within the Frontier markets sleeve, the weight of any one security may not go beyond 10% of net asset value; this operates in addition to the whole-Fund cap described above rather than in place of it.

Sector exposure is constrained by reference to particular sectors. Holdings classified within the Technology sector may not together represent more than 30% of net asset value, and holdings classified within the Materials sector may not together represent more than 20% of net asset value.

Geographic exposure is constrained by domicile and by region. Issuers domiciled in India may not together account for more than 25% of net asset value; issuers domiciled in Brazil may not together account for more than 15% of net asset value; and the Fund's aggregate exposure to frontier markets, taken as a whole, may not go beyond 10% of net asset value.

Separately from all of the above, there is an absolute prohibition. Where an issuer appears on the Firm's Restricted List, no security of that issuer may be bought and no existing holding in it may be increased, whatever the circumstances. Neither the Manager, nor the Chief Investment Officer, nor Compliance is able to waive this prohibition, and no committee or other individual has authority to set it aside.

As a matter of background rather than rule, readers may find it useful to know that the Manager's typical initial position size is approximately 2.5% of net asset value. That is a description of how positions tend to be initiated and nothing more; a position opened at a different size does not offend any provision of this document.

Exceptions to the concentration, sector, country and sleeve limits are possible, but only with the joint approval of the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance. A request must be put in writing, must explain why the exception is needed and how compliance will be restored, and must be entered by Compliance in the exceptions register. An exception lapses after 30 days at the latest. The Restricted List prohibition admits no exception whatsoever.

If a breach occurs, Compliance informs the portfolio manager and the Chief Investment Officer promptly. The portfolio manager then puts forward a remediation plan, and Compliance records the breach, its cause and its resolution in the breach log. A breach that is material, or that has not been cured by the end of its remediation plan, is escalated to the Board.

The Chief Investment Officer is the owner of these Guidelines and the Board approves each edition. The document is revisited annually and whenever a change in the prospectus, the benchmark or the Fund's regulatory position bears on any of the limits described. Records relating to its operation are kept in line with the Manager's record-keeping policy.
