# Midland Emerging Markets Fund: Investment Guidelines and Restrictions

Version 2, effective 2026-04-01

These Investment Guidelines and Restrictions (the "Guidelines") govern the construction of the portfolio of the Midland Emerging Markets Fund (the "Fund") and supplement its Investment Policy Statement. They have been approved by the Board and bind the Manager, the portfolio management team and every delegate acting for the Fund. Should a provision of these Guidelines and a provision of the Investment Policy Statement address the same subject, the more restrictive of the two applies.

The headline limit on individual holdings is straightforward: no single security shall exceed 5% of net asset value. This is the whole-Fund limit and it is tested against every proposed purchase and every addition to an existing holding. The proviso that modifies it for certain benchmark constituents is set out later in this document, after the issuer and sector provisions, and readers should take care to read the two together.

At the issuer level, aggregate exposure to any single issuer, including all securities of that issuer, shall not exceed 8% of net asset value. The aggregation gathers together ordinary shares, preference shares, depositary receipts and any other instrument whose value is derived from the issuer, so that the issuer limit cannot be circumvented by holding the same economic exposure through different instruments.

Sector exposure is limited as follows. Exposure to the Technology sector shall not exceed 30% of net asset value, and exposure to the Materials sector shall not exceed 20% of net asset value. Sector classification follows the benchmark provider's methodology unless the Valuation Committee has determined that a different classification is appropriate for a particular security.

Returning to the single security limit described above, that limit applies unless the security represents more than 4% of the benchmark, in which case the limit is the benchmark weight plus 2 percentage points. The alternative limit is available only while the benchmark condition continues to hold; once the security's benchmark weight falls to or below the condition, the headline limit of the earlier paragraph applies again without further action. The proviso modifies the whole-Fund single security limit only and has no bearing on the issuer limit, the sector limits or the sleeve limit.

Country exposure is limited as follows. Exposure to issuers domiciled in India shall not exceed 25% of net asset value, and exposure to issuers domiciled in Brazil shall not exceed 15% of net asset value. Domicile follows the benchmark provider's methodology subject to the same Valuation Committee override that applies to sector classification.

The Fund may invest in frontier markets through a dedicated sleeve, and that sleeve is subject to its own rules. Within the Frontier markets sleeve, no single security shall exceed 10% of net asset value, a limit that operates alongside rather than instead of the whole-Fund single security limit. In addition, aggregate exposure to frontier markets shall not exceed 10% of net asset value.

Quite separately from the limits described so far, there is one absolute prohibition. No security of an issuer appearing on the Firm's Restricted List may be purchased or added to under any circumstances. This restriction cannot be waived by the Manager, the Chief Investment Officer or Compliance, and no other individual or committee has authority to override it. Trading must consult the Restricted List before any order is released.

For the information of readers, and not as a rule of any kind, the Manager's typical initial position size is approximately 2.5% of net asset value. That figure describes how positions have usually been initiated. It is neither a floor nor a ceiling, and a position opened at a different size does not offend any provision of these Guidelines.

All limits are measured on a post-trade basis, meaning the existing exposure plus the change a proposed transaction would produce. Passive movements arising from market prices, corporate actions or investor dealing are not breaches in themselves, but once such a movement is identified the portfolio manager must restore compliance in an orderly manner and without disadvantaging investors.

An exception to any concentration, sector, country or sleeve limit may be approved only by the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Compliance acting together. Requests must be made in writing, must set out the rationale and the intended route back to compliance, and must be entered by Compliance in the exceptions register. No exception may remain in force for longer than 30 days. No exception of any kind is available in respect of the Restricted List.

When a breach is identified, Compliance notifies the portfolio manager and the Chief Investment Officer without delay. The portfolio manager proposes a remediation plan, and Compliance records the breach, its cause and its resolution in the breach log. Material breaches, and any breach that outlives its remediation plan, are reported to the Board.

These Guidelines are owned by the Chief Investment Officer and approved by the Board. They are reviewed annually and whenever a change to the prospectus, the benchmark or the Fund's regulatory status affects any limit above. Records supporting their operation are retained in accordance with the Manager's record-keeping policy.
