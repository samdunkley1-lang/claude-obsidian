# Midland Emerging Markets Fund: Liquidity Risk Management Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

This Liquidity Risk Management Policy (the "Policy") describes how the Midland Emerging Markets Fund (the "Fund") manages the risk that it cannot meet redemption requests, or cannot rebalance its portfolio, without disadvantaging investors. It binds the Manager, the portfolio management team, Risk and any delegate acting for the Fund, and it operates alongside the Investment Policy Statement and the Investment Guidelines and Restrictions. Where those documents and this Policy address the same matter, the more restrictive provision applies.

For the purposes of this Policy, a holding is classified as illiquid where Risk has determined, using the Manager's approved liquidity model and its own judgement, that the holding could not be realised at or near its carrying value within a reasonable period under normal market conditions. A holding is treated as realisable within a given number of business days where the model indicates that the Fund's entire position could be sold within that period at a participation rate that would not be expected to move the market price materially. Classifications are refreshed whenever the model inputs are updated and, in any event, whenever Risk considers that market conditions have changed.

The first binding requirement concerns the size of the illiquid book. Holdings classified as illiquid shall not exceed 10% of net asset value. This limit is tested on a post-trade basis, so that a proposed purchase is assessed by adding its effect to the existing illiquid exposure and comparing the result with the limit. A holding that becomes illiquid through reclassification rather than through purchase does not create a breach at the moment of reclassification, but the portfolio manager must restore compliance in an orderly manner once Risk has notified the change.

The second requirement concerns the portfolio as a whole. At least 70% of net asset value shall be realisable within five business days under normal market conditions. Risk measures this figure using the approved liquidity model and reports it to the portfolio manager and the Chief Investment Officer on each business day. Where a proposed transaction would take the realisable proportion below the floor, the transaction may not proceed.

The third requirement concerns dealing. Swing pricing shall be applied on any dealing day on which net flows exceed 2.5% of net asset value. The Fund administrator calculates net flows for each dealing day and applies the swing factor approved by the Valuation Committee whenever the threshold is crossed. The portfolio manager has no discretion to disapply swing pricing on a day on which the threshold has been met.

The fourth requirement concerns individual positions and is expressed in days to liquidate. No single position shall require more than 5 business days to liquidate at 25% of average daily volume. Risk calculates days to liquidate for every position using trailing average daily volume from the approved market data source. A position whose days to liquidate exceeds the limit is reported to the portfolio manager, who must set out how and when the position will be brought back within the limit.

For context, and without imposing any limit, at the last review illiquid holdings represented approximately 3% of net asset value. That is a description of the portfolio as it stood at the review date; the binding limit on illiquid holdings remains that stated above, and the review figure has no bearing on whether any transaction is permitted.

An exception to any of the requirements in this Policy may be approved only by the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk acting together. A request must be made in writing, must set out the rationale and the intended route back to compliance, and must be entered by Compliance in the exceptions register. No exception may remain in force for longer than 30 days.

When a breach is identified, Compliance notifies the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk without delay. The portfolio manager proposes a remediation plan, and Compliance records the breach, its cause and its resolution in the breach log. Material breaches, and any breach that outlives its remediation plan, are reported to the Board.

This Policy is owned by the Chief Investment Officer, with Risk responsible for its day-to-day operation, and it is approved by the Board. It is reviewed annually and whenever a change to the prospectus, the dealing arrangements or the regulatory status of the Fund affects any requirement above. Liquidity model outputs, classification decisions, swing pricing calculations and exception records are retained in accordance with the Manager's record-keeping policy.
