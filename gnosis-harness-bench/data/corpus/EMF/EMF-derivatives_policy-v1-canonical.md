# Midland Emerging Markets Fund: Derivatives Usage Policy

Version 1, effective 2025-01-01

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This Derivatives Usage Policy (the "Policy") governs every use of derivative instruments by or on behalf of the Midland Emerging Markets Fund (the "Fund"). It applies to exchange-traded and over-the-counter instruments alike, including futures, options, forwards, swaps and any contract whose value is derived from an underlying security, index, currency or rate.

1.2. The Policy binds the Manager, the portfolio management team, the dealing desk and any delegate acting for the Fund. It supplements the Investment Policy Statement and the Counterparty and Broker Policy, and where those documents and this Policy address the same matter the more restrictive provision applies.

1.3. Derivatives may be used for efficient portfolio management, for hedging currency and market risk, and for gaining exposure to markets where direct access is impractical. Derivatives may not be used to create leverage beyond the limits set out in clause 3.

## 2. Definitions

2.1. "Gross notional exposure" means the sum of the absolute notional values of all derivative positions held by the Fund, without netting between positions.

2.2. "Mark-to-market exposure" means the current replacement value of all derivative contracts with a counterparty, net of collateral received from that counterparty under an enforceable arrangement.

2.3. "Uncollateralised derivatives exposure" means mark-to-market exposure to a counterparty that is not covered by eligible collateral held by or for the Fund.

2.4. "Net asset value" has the meaning given in the Fund's prospectus.

## 3. Exposure limits

3.1. Gross notional exposure through derivatives shall not exceed 15% of net asset value.

3.2. Mark-to-market exposure to any single derivatives counterparty shall not exceed 15% of net asset value.

3.3. Uncollateralised derivatives exposure shall not exceed 5% of net asset value, measured across all counterparties in aggregate.

3.4. Each limit in this clause is tested on a post-trade basis, meaning the existing exposure together with the change a proposed transaction would produce. Passive movements in exposure arising from market prices do not of themselves constitute a breach, but the portfolio manager must restore compliance in an orderly manner once such a movement has been identified.

## 4. Counterparty eligibility

4.1. Derivatives may only be transacted with counterparties rated BBB+ or better by at least one recognised rating agency.

4.2. Where a derivatives counterparty is downgraded below BBB+ after a contract has been entered into, no new transaction may be entered into with that counterparty, and the portfolio manager shall, in consultation with Risk, determine whether existing contracts should be novated, closed out or allowed to run to maturity.

4.3. Every derivatives counterparty must be approved by Risk and added to the approved counterparty list before the first transaction with it, and must be party to appropriate master documentation.

## 5. Historical context

5.1. For the information of readers, and without creating any limit or expectation, over the last three years gross notional derivatives exposure has typically been around 6% of net asset value. That figure records past practice; the binding limit remains that set out in clause 3.

## 6. Exceptions

6.1. An exception to any limit in clause 3 or clause 4 may be approved only by the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk, acting together.

6.2. A request for an exception must be in writing, must state the rationale and the intended route back to compliance, and must be recorded by Compliance in the exceptions register.

6.3. No exception may remain in force for longer than 30 days.

## 7. Breach procedure

7.1. Compliance notifies the portfolio manager, the Chief Investment Officer and Risk as soon as a breach is identified.

7.2. The portfolio manager proposes a remediation plan, and Compliance records the breach, its cause and its resolution in the breach log.

7.3. Material breaches, and any breach that remains unresolved once its remediation plan has run its course, are reported to the Board.

## 8. Governance, review and records

8.1. This Policy is owned by the Chief Investment Officer and approved by the Board.

8.2. It is reviewed annually and whenever a change to the prospectus, the regulatory status of the Fund or the Manager's counterparty framework affects any provision above.

8.3. Confirmations, collateral records, master agreements and exposure reports are retained in accordance with the Manager's record-keeping policy.
