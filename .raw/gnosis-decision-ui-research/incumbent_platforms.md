## Key Question 1: Order/portfolio management and front-office platforms — how each handles rule authoring, breach/exception routing, audit trail and point-in-time reconstruction

### Takeaway
Every incumbent OMS ships a large template library for pre-trade guideline rules plus an event-driven exception workflow (block, alert, supervisor approve/reject), but public material describes rule-change governance and exception queues rather than decision-level rationale capture; only Charles River ("As-Of Compliance") and Bloomberg ("on-demand compliance snapshots") publicly market anything resembling point-in-time reconstruction, and none describe bitemporal replay of a decision with its inputs, rule version and approvals as of time T.

### Inferences
- Across Aladdin, Charles River, Bloomberg and Eze the dominant pattern is "template library + coded rules + exception blotter": rules are authored by specialist coders or compliance analysts from templates, and the audit trail is centered on the rule change and the exception's lifecycle, not on the investment decision's rationale, inputs or precedents.
- The only public "as-of" claims (Charles River As-Of Compliance; Bloomberg on-demand compliance snapshots) appear to be evaluations of positions/rules as of a date rather than bitemporal replay of a decision with its approvals and evidence; this is inferred from product naming, since neither page could be read in full.
- SimCorp's documented four-eyes limitation (change is live before approval) suggests that even mature incumbents implement segregation of duties as post-hoc review of data changes rather than a hard gate on state transitions.

## Key Question 2: Compliance and surveillance vendors — coverage and where they stop

### Takeaway
ACA, MCO, Confluence/CSS and FundApps cover employee conduct (personal trading, pre-clearance, insider lists), regulatory reporting/disclosure and marketing review; Eventus, NICE Actimize, Behavox, Smarsh and Global Relay cover trade and communications surveillance. None of them positions itself as the system of record for investment-decision rationale, mandate/IPS evaluation at decision time, or approval chains on discretionary investment decisions.

### Inferences
- The employee-conduct vendors (ACA, MCO) stop at the person and the trade ticket (pre-clearance, attestations, surveillance); the disclosure/reporting vendors (FundApps, Confluence/CSS) stop at position thresholds and filings; the surveillance vendors stop at detecting misconduct after the fact. The "why was this investment decision made, under which mandate version, approved by whom, with which precedents" record sits in none of them.
- FundApps's explicit "AI beside the rules engine, not inside it, human makes the final call" and Eventus's "deterministic, explainable" framing indicate the buyer expectation gnosis must meet: deterministic policy evaluation with AI confined to assistance.

## Key Question 3: Four-eyes, maker-checker and dual-approval features and their configurability

### Takeaway
Four-eyes controls exist in the incumbents mainly on rule/static-data changes (Charles River rule creation, SimCorp reference data) and on exception approvals (Bloomberg CMGR electronic approval, Aladdin exception assignment), and they are configurable at field or rule level; no source describes chained, role-based approval on the investment decision itself, and SimCorp documents that unapproved changes are live before the second pair of eyes signs off.

### Cited Findings
- Charles River: rule creation from templates includes "four eyes" approval in the compliance workspace (Feb 2026 brochure) — [CRD Charles River Compliance brochure](https://www.crd.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CRD_Charles-River-Compliance.pdf); corporate-action elections carry four-eyes verification with audit trails on elections and approvals — [Charles River Corporate Actions](https://info.crd.com/corporate-actions)
- SimCorp Dimension: four-eyes approval is driven off the audit trail, configurable per field, but there is "no concept of active/inactive data," so a change is effective before approval (third-party consultant note) — [Dimensional Community Tip #20](https://www.dimensionalcommunity.com/insights-articles/tip-20-four-eyes-approval)
- Bloomberg CMGR: pre-violation workflow includes "electronic approval," and compliance supervisors "review and approve or reject blocked trades on an event-driven basis" — [WatersTechnology BST Awards 2025](https://www.waterstechnology.com/awards-rankings/7952788/bst-awards-2025-best-buy-side-compliance-product-trading-bloomberg); [Bloomberg compliance page](https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/compliance/)
- Aladdin: exception resolution workflow lets firms assign responsibility, comment and age exceptions — [Aladdin compliance benefits](https://www.blackrock.com/aladdin/benefits/compliance)
- Generic framing of the four-eyes principle in regulated firms (context only) — [Mondaq: What is the four-eyes principle](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1559702/what-is-the-four-eyes-principle-that-regulated-companies-need-to-adhere-to)

### Inferences
- Configurability is at the data-object or rule level (which fields, which rule) rather than at the decision-type level; a schema-driven decision type with its own approval chain (gnosis's model) has no documented equivalent in the retrieved material.
- No incumbent source describes gates that administrators cannot override (information-barrier or conflict-of-interest hard gates); the SimCorp behavior is the opposite (change first, approve later). This is an absence in the retrieved sources, not proof the feature is missing.

### Gaps
- No documentation retrieved for Eze, Enfusion, Aladdin or Bloomberg on whether rule changes themselves require dual approval, on override permissions, or on N-level approval chains; vendor manuals are behind login.

### Inferences
- Cannot beat: (1) pre-trade rule breadth and the coded-rule libraries built over decades; (2) real-time market/reference data and OMS/EMS order-routing integration; (3) the "single platform" claim (risk, PM, trading, compliance) that Aladdin, Charles River, SimCorp and Clearwater/Enfusion all make; (4) installed base and marketplace distribution (Eze Marketplace, SimCorp Agent Launchpad, Aladdin Studio).
- Can beat: (1) structured, schema-driven capture of the decision proposal, inputs and rationale (incumbents capture exceptions, not decisions); (2) precedent retrieval across prior decisions (no vendor claims this); (3) bitemporal replay of a decision with rule version, inputs and approvals as of time T (Charles River's "As-Of Compliance" and Bloomberg's snapshots are the closest analogues and are position/rule-centric); (4) hard gates that admins cannot override (no incumbent evidence; SimCorp's model is the opposite); (5) explainable, deterministic policy evaluation with a validated recommendation engine and hard-gated learning loop, matching the FundApps/Eventus framing buyers already accept; (6) agent lineage — every read-only tool call logged against the decision, which aligns with the MCP FSIG agenda that Bloomberg is driving.
- Must plug into: Aladdin Data Cloud / Aladdin Graph and Trading APIs via AladdinSDK; State Street Alpha Data Platform (Snowflake); SimCorp Data/Integration/Streaming APIs; Snowflake Marketplace and Databricks Delta Sharing for data; FactSet and LSEG MCP endpoints for market/reference context; Bloomberg Enterprise MCP once client-available; and, for exceptions that must block orders, the OMS's own exception workflow (CMGR pre-violation approval, Aladdin exception assignment) rather than a parallel blocking path.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head analyst evaluation (Celent, Datos, Chartis, Gartner) of decision-rationale or audit-trail depth across these platforms was accessible; the "can beat" list is inferred from absence in marketing and documentation summaries, not from product testing.
- Whether Aladdin Copilot's 2026 agentic upgrades include decision-support records or exception-rationale capture (the March 2026 report mentions "exception management, compliance interpretation, and portfolio decision support") could not be verified beyond that sentence.
