## Key Question 1: Enumerate the decision types (who decides, inputs/constraints, approval path, what regulators/auditors expect recorded, frequency, current-process pain)

### Takeaway
The regulatory and enforcement record shows that regulators already treat a specific subset of buy-side decisions as "must be recorded with rationale and evidence": fair-value marks (SEC Rule 2a-5 / 31a-4; FCA March 2025 review), liquidity-tool and gating decisions (FCA July 2023 review; Woodford notices), trade allocation (Western Asset), model changes (Two Sigma), ESG screen application (DWS/BNY/Goldman), and all written communications relating to recommendations or advice (Advisers Act Rule 204-2(a)(7); AIFMD Level 2 Arts. 64–66). The rest of the taxonomy (allocation, sizing, hedging, corporate-action elections, etc.) is governed mainly by internal mandate/IPS rules and is documented inconsistently; the per-type attributes below are therefore a mix of sourced anchors and clearly-labelled inference.

### Cited Findings

**Valuation / pricing-committee decisions (Level 3, stale prices, odd lots)**
- SEC adopted Rule 2a-5 on 3 December 2020; a fund board may designate a "valuation designee" (typically the adviser) to make fair-value determinations subject to board oversight and reporting; the board should receive quarterly reporting of material changes in valuation risks or methodologies and an annual assessment of the adequacy and effectiveness of the designee's process — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/library/sec-good-faith-determinations-of-fair-value.html); [Vedder Price](https://www.vedderprice.com/sec-adopts-new-framework-for-fund-valuation)
- Companion Rule 31a-4 requires funds or advisers to maintain documentation supporting fair-value determinations, generally for six years with the first two in an easily accessible place — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/library/sec-good-faith-determinations-of-fair-value.html)
- FCA published its multi-firm review of private-market valuation practices on 6 March 2025 after surveying 36 UK firms (PE, VC, private debt, infrastructure) in two phases including on-site visits; it reported on eight areas: governance arrangements, conflicts of interest, functional independence and expertise, policies/procedures/documentation, frequency and ad-hoc valuations, transparency to investors, application of methodologies, and third-party valuation services; overall verdict "good practice but room for improvement" — [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/private-market-valuation-practices); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/fca-review-of-private-market-valuation-practices); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/03/fca-findings-on-private-market-valuations)
- The FCA said the findings will inform its forthcoming review of UK AIFMD — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/fcas-private-market-valuations-review-good-practice-but-room-for-improvement)
- SEC v. Macquarie Investment Management Business Trust (settled 2024, $79.8m = $70m penalty + $9.8m disgorgement/interest): approximately 4,900 largely illiquid odd-lot CMOs across 20 accounts (including 11 retail mutual funds) were valued using third-party pricing-service prices intended for institutional lots, Jan 2017–Apr 2021; the firm also executed 465 internal cross trades and ~175 dealer-interposed cross trades that disadvantaged the mutual funds — [SEC press release 2024-140](https://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-140); [The DESK](https://www.fi-desk.com/sec-fines-mimbt-79-8m-for-overvaluing-4900-cmos/)
- SEC v. Sciens (May 2023, $275,000): charged under the compliance rule for failing to implement written valuation policies reasonably designed for funds investing in assets with no readily available pricing; policies "provided only minimal guidance" on GAAP valuation and "lacked procedures designed to promote consistency in the valuation process" — [Cleary Enforcement Watch, Mar 2026](https://www.clearyenforcementwatch.com/2026/03/enforcers-target-fund-valuation-practices/)
- SEC v. Madison Capital Funding LLC (25 Feb 2026): settled charges that the (formerly registered) adviser sold loans to private-fund clients representing they were priced at fair market value without having taken certain steps; the SEC brought the case even though the funds had been made whole and the adviser had de-registered — [Cleary Enforcement Watch](https://www.clearyenforcementwatch.com/2026/03/enforcers-target-fund-valuation-practices/)

**Liquidity management, gating, swing pricing**
- FCA's July 2023 multi-firm review of 14 authorised fund managers assessed governance, liquidity stress testing, redemption processes, liquidity management tools and valuation; it found many firms "attach insufficient weight to liquidity risk management in their governance oversight arrangements" and provide "insufficient challenge and escalation, particularly in volatile environments", while firms with a separate liquidity risk committee "did a far better job" — [FCA review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/liquidity-management-multi-firm-review); [FCA Dear CEO letter 6 Jul 2023](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/correspondence/dear-ceo-letter-liquidity-management-multi-firm-review.pdf); [Norton Rose Fulbright summary](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2023/07/fca-publishes-results-of-multi-firm-review-of-liquidity-management-in-funds/)
- Same review: building blocks were in place but "lack coherence when viewed as a full process"; firms could handle large one-off redemptions but not cumulative/market-wide redemptions; "wide variations in the application of anti-dilution tools such as swing pricing" — [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102iis5/fca-tells-asset-managers-to-review-their-liquidity-management-arrangements/); [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/asset-managers-told-review-liquidity-management-funds)
- FCA Decision Notices of 1 July 2025: Neil Woodford fined £5,888,800 and banned from senior-manager roles/managing retail funds; Woodford Investment Management fined £40,000,000; conduct: between 31 July 2018 and 3 June 2019, as lead fund manager and risk owner, he failed to exercise due skill, care and diligence in managing the fund's liquidity risk, leading to an inappropriate liquidity profile, suspension and investor losses (total nearly £46m) — [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-over-woodford-equity-income-fund); [Decision Notice – Neil Woodford](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/decision-notices/neil-woodford-2025.pdf); [Decision Notice – WIM](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/decision-notices/woodford-investment-management-ltd-2025.pdf)
- Link Fund Solutions (the ACD) was found to have failed to manage the fund's liquidity so that investors could access money at short notice over the same window; it settled earlier (FCA findings April 2024) — [FCA findings on LFS](https://www.wired-gov.net/wg/news.nsf/articles/FCA+sets+out+findings+against+Link+Fund+Solutions+12042024092500)

**Trade allocation (a decision type the assignment did not list but enforcement makes central)**
- SEC v. Western Asset Management (2024, $100m civil penalty) and its former co-CIO Stephen Kenneth Leech: "cherry-picking" by delaying allocation instructions until after observing intraday moves, Jan 2021–Oct 2023; trading began as early as 5:00 am PT but allocation instructions were routinely entered after noon; first-day gains were steered to Macro Opportunities portfolios and losses to Core/Core Plus, allegedly funnelling >$600m of profits — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/mutual-funds/western-asset-agrees-to-100m-sec-penalty-over-cherry-picking-scheme/266892); [SEC complaint](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2024/comp-pr2024-187.pdf)
- Leech pleaded guilty (June 2026) to obstructing the probe, for lying in March 2024 sworn SEC testimony when asked whether he had "an allocation in mind" when placing trades — [CNBC, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/kenneth-leech-pleads-guilty-to-obstructing-cherry-picking-probe.html)

**Model changes / model validation sign-off**
- SEC v. Two Sigma Investments LP and Two Sigma Advisers LP (16 Jan 2025, $90m penalties; $165m voluntarily repaid): employees identified vulnerabilities in investment models in or before March 2019 but the firm did not address them until August 2023; employees had "read/write" access to modify models without proper oversight; one researcher's unauthorised changes caused ~ $165m underperformance in some funds and >$400m outperformance in others; charges included fiduciary breach plus compliance and supervisory failures and a whistleblower-rule violation (separation agreements Apr 2019–Feb 2024) — [SEC press release 2025-15](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15); [P&I](https://www.pionline.com/hedge-funds/two-sigma-settles-sec-charges-over-vulnerabilities-investment-models-pays-90-million/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-16/two-sigma-to-pay-sec-90-million-after-investment-model-lapse)

**ESG exclusions / screen application**
- DWS paid $19m (Sept 2023) — described as the largest greenwashing penalty to date — for failing, between Aug 2018 and late 2021, to implement controls for incorporating ESG factors into research and recommendations as represented, and failing to ensure public statements were accurate; BNY Mellon Investment Adviser paid $1.5m (May 2022) for misstating how it applied ESG criteria in investment decisions for some mutual funds; Goldman Sachs Asset Management paid $4m (Nov 2022) for failing to follow its ESG policies and procedures — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/deutsche-dws-25-million-esg-aml-penalty-sec-fixler-hoops-wohrmann-misstatement-goldman-bny/694709/); [Responsible Investor](https://www.responsible-investor.com/dws-to-settle-with-sec-for-19m-over-greenwashing-claims/)
- Arnold & Porter's reading of these cases: the SEC focused on lack of (1) training, (2) testing, (3) controls, (4) standards for management, (5) consistency in approach, and (6) remediation once inconsistencies were recognised — [CLS Blue Sky Blog, Oct 2023](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2023/10/19/arnold-porter-discusses-esg-whistleblowing-and-compliance-programs)
- DWS was separately fined €25m by BaFin (April 2025) over greenwashing allegations — [D&O Diary](https://www.dandodiary.com/2025/04/articles/esg/deutsche-bank-asset-management-unit-pays-e25m-greenwashing-fine/)

**Security selection and sizing (governance model at a leading firm)**
- Capital Group's "Capital System" divides each portfolio into segments run by individual portfolio managers; "while there may be group discussions, there are no group decisions"; analysts run a "research portfolio" of generally ~20% of assets; "a multilayered governance structure oversees the System" — [Capital Group](https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/about-us/capital-system.html); [Canada Life summary](https://www.canadalife.com/investment-management/news-insights/capital-groups-multi-manager-approach.html); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/capital-group-multimanager-system-best-both-worlds)

**Portfolio-transaction and order records (EU/UK AIFMs)**
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 231/2013 (AIFMD Level 2), Chapter III Section 6: Art. 64 recording of portfolio transactions; Art. 65 recording of subscription and redemption orders; Art. 66 record-keeping, retained at least five years — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2013/231/contents); [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/publications/aifmd/liquidity-management-and-other-operating-conditions)

**Written communications about recommendations/advice (US advisers)**
- Advisers Act Rule 204-2(a)(7) requires RIAs to preserve originals of written communications received and copies sent relating to, among other things, any recommendations made or proposed and any advice given or proposed — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/whatsapp-all-over-again-the-sec-brings-more-recordkeeping-charges-against-broker-dealers-and-investment-advisers-for-off-channel-communications)

- Inference on priority for a product: the decision types with both (a) an explicit regulatory record standard and (b) recent large penalties are valuation marks, liquidity tools, trade allocation, model changes and ESG screen application. ## Key Question 2: Which decision types are most commonly cited as under-documented, and where have regulators fined firms for missing decision records (2020–2026)?

### Takeaway
The largest 2020–2026 penalties tied to decision records fall into five clusters: off-channel communications about advice (~$2.7bn across ~60 firms since 2021), trade-allocation timing (Western Asset $100m), model-change governance (Two Sigma $90m), valuation of illiquid assets (Macquarie $79.8m; Sciens; Madison Capital) and liquidity management (Woodford/WIM ~£46m; Link). Regulators' language in these cases centres on absence of contemporaneous, time-stamped intent (allocation), absence of change control and remediation records (models), policies "lacking procedures to promote consistency" (valuation), and "insufficient challenge and escalation" (liquidity).

### Inferences
- The common thread is that regulators penalise the absence of a record that binds intent, timing and approver: allocation intent at order entry (Western Asset), who changed a model and who approved it (Two Sigma), why a mark was chosen and by whom (Macquarie/Sciens), and who challenged and escalated a liquidity decision (FCA/Woodford). A sealed commit with actor, timestamp, rationale and approval chain maps directly onto each.
- Off-channel enforcement implies that any decision discussion held in chat/email outside retained channels is itself a compliance exposure — a structured decision ledger reduces the amount of decision content that lives in unretained messaging.
- Exit decisions and position sizing are the most under-documented decision types by behavioural evidence (Q6) but have attracted no enforcement; they are a performance problem, not a compliance problem.

## Key Question 3: How do investment committees at leading firms operate (memos, pre-mortems, dissent, decision rights, quorum, minutes, follow-up), and what do Bridgewater, Capital Group, Baillie Gifford, Man Group, Two Sigma, Wellington publish?

### Takeaway
Publicly documented models split into two archetypes: (1) Bridgewater's committee-style "believability-weighted" voting with explicit disagreement tooling (Dot Collector, Baseball Cards), and (2) Capital Group's and Baillie Gifford's individual-accountability models where discussion is collective but decisions and their records are owned by named PMs using structured research-question templates. Man Group publishes a skill-measurement/behavioural-feedback framework aimed at PMs. Wellington and Two Sigma publish nothing specific about IC mechanics that I could locate.

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 4: Decision-quality and decision-governance frameworks a "clinical" product should embody (with specific concepts)

### Takeaway
Six frameworks supply distinct, mappable concepts: Spetzler's six-link Decision Quality chain; Kahneman-Sibony-Sunstein's decision hygiene (noise audit, independent judgments, Mediating Assessments Protocol); Duke's separation of decision quality from outcome ("resulting") via decision journals; Mauboussin's base rates, reference classes and journaling; Klein's pre-mortem (prospective hindsight); and Bridgewater's believability weighting. Each is sourced below with its operative concepts.

### Cited Findings
- Spetzler (Strategic Decisions Group) — six elements of decision quality: appropriate frame; creative/doable alternatives; meaningful, reliable information (including about uncertainty); clear values and trade-offs; sound reasoning; commitment to action; "a decision is like a chain: its quality equals the weakest of the six links" — [CBS News interview](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/carl-spetzler-the-six-elements-of-a-good-decision/); [Argumentree summary](https://www.argumentree.com/blog/decision-quality-chain/); book: Spetzler, Winter, Meyer, *Decision Quality* (Wiley, 2016) — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Decision+Quality:+Value+Creation+from+Better+Business+Decisions-p-9781119176657)
- Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein, *Noise* (published 18 May 2021): "decision hygiene" as hand-washing-like practices that reduce noise without visible payoff; a "noise audit" quantifies unwanted variability in judgments to set a baseline; the Mediating Assessments Protocol (MAP) "separates evaluation into independent components before combining them into a final decision" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise:_A_Flaw_in_Human_Judgment); [The Uncertainty Project: MAP](https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/the-mediating-assessments-protocol); [To Summarise: decision hygiene](https://www.tosummarise.com/decision-hygiene/)
- Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem", HBR Sept 2007: team assumes the project has already failed and generates plausible reasons; based on 1989 research finding that prospective hindsight increases ability to correctly identify reasons for future outcomes by 30%; explicitly aimed at letting dissenters speak at the outset — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem); [PDF copy](http://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2013/01/Performing-a-Project-Premortem.pdf)
- Annie Duke: "resulting" = judging decision quality by outcome; the decision journal exists to separate decision quality from outcome quality so process, not outcomes, is measured — [Crucible: decision journals](https://readcrucible.com/articles/decision-journals-the-practice-the-science-the-templates); [Duke, Re:Think Decision Making 2019 slides](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/learningcommunity/Annie+Duke_RTDM_3889.pdf)
- Farnam Street (Shane Parrish) decision-journal template fields: situation/context, options considered, decision made, expected outcome, and a later post-mortem asking what was expected, what happened, and why the gap — [Farnam Street (X post with template)](https://x.com/farnamstreet/status/1530146185952038918); [Alvin Alexander example](https://alvinalexander.com/personal/decision-journal-template-example/); [Ben Meer](https://benmeer.com/newsletter/decision-journal/)
- Mauboussin (Head of Consilient Research, Counterpoint Global, Morgan Stanley IM): emphasises base rates and reference classes, "the undervalued impact of keeping a decision journal", and the role of luck; a short-term favourable outcome can be an unreliable indicator of process quality; recommended decision aids: base rates, pre-mortems, red teaming, journaling — [Alliance for Decision Education podcast ep. 1](https://alliancefordecisioneducation.org/podcasts/episode-001-base-rates-and-bees-michael-mauboussin/); [Alpha Theory on Mauboussin's two decision aids](https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/mauboussin-the-two-decision-aids-that-fundamental-investors-should-use-but-often-dont); [Counterpoint Global: Bayes and Base Rates](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_bayesandbaserates_ltr.pdf); [Counterpoint Global: Feedback](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_feedback_us.pdf)
- Bridgewater believability weighting: see Q3 — [Principles](https://www.principles.com/principles/633d5d13-8610-425f-ad62-cd62347d9165/)
- Alpha Theory (vendor research, 2023 year in review): systematic "optimal" sizing built from the manager's own price targets/probabilities beat actual sizing in 11 of 12 years (92% win rate), compound 268% vs 130% actual vs 94% average hedge fund; "the more time spent applying process, the more likely the process is to win" — [Alpha Theory](https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2023-year-in-review)

### Inferences
Feature-to-concept mapping (my synthesis):
- Typed decision schema with required inputs → Spetzler "appropriate frame" + "meaningful, reliable information"; MAP's decomposition into independent assessments.
- Alternatives field (what else was considered, incl. "do nothing") → Spetzler "creative alternatives".
- Policy evaluation against mandate/IPS → Spetzler "clear values and trade-offs" (constraints made explicit).
- Chained four-eyes approvals with recorded dissent and weights → Bridgewater believability weighting; Noise "aggregate independent judgments" (approvers should record their view before seeing others').
- Precedent linking → Mauboussin/Tetlock reference classes and base rates ("how did decisions of this type turn out before?").
- Pre-commit "how could this fail" prompt → Klein pre-mortem.
- Sealed commit with expected outcome + review date → Duke/Parrish decision journal, enabling process-vs-outcome scoring and noise audits across PMs later.
- Noise audit as a product analytic: if several PMs face the same decision type with the same inputs, variance in their sizing/marks is measurable noise.

### Gaps
- Tetlock/superforecasting (Brier scores, calibration training, "ten commandments") and Duke's *Quit* "kill criteria" (states-and-dates) could not be sourced before the search budget ran out; these are well-known but unverified here — the writer should cite the books directly.
- The exact MAP step list and the noise taxonomy (level/pattern/occasion noise) were not retrievable; the Wikipedia/Uncertainty Project pages above should be read for the step list.
- No CFA Institute guidance document specifically on investment-process documentation was located (only the governance monograph). CFA Standard V(C) "Record Retention" is a likely lead but was not searched.

## Key Question 5: What fields does a "complete" decision record contain according to these frameworks and to auditors/regulators?

### Takeaway
Combining the frameworks' journal fields with what regulators demanded in the 2020–2026 cases yields a record with three layers: (a) journal layer — situation, alternatives, decision, rationale, expected outcome, confidence, review date, post-mortem; (b) governance layer — decider, approvers, dissent (weighted), policy checks and exceptions, timestamp of intent; (c) evidence layer — inputs and sources with timestamps, methodology, third-party data, change log. The regulatory anchors are strongest for valuation (31a-4 six-year documentation), portfolio transactions (AIFMD Art. 64) and communications about advice (Rule 204-2(a)(7)).

### Cited Findings
- Journal fields (situation, options, decision, expected outcome, post-mortem gap analysis) — [Farnam Street](https://x.com/farnamstreet/status/1530146185952038918); [Crucible](https://readcrucible.com/articles/decision-journals-the-practice-the-science-the-templates)
- Decision-quality elements to evidence (frame, alternatives, information incl. uncertainty, values/trade-offs, reasoning, commitment) — [CBS/Spetzler](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/carl-spetzler-the-six-elements-of-a-good-decision/)
- Base rates / reference class and journaling — [Alliance for Decision Education](https://alliancefordecisioneducation.org/podcasts/episode-001-base-rates-and-bees-michael-mauboussin/)
- Pre-mortem reasons-for-failure list — [HBR/Klein](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)
- Independent assessments before aggregation (MAP) — [Uncertainty Project](https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/the-mediating-assessments-protocol)
- Weighted votes and each person's vote displayed — [Principles: Dot Collector](https://www.principles.com/principles/3290232e-6bca-4585-a4f6-66874aefce30)
- Valuation: documentation supporting each fair-value determination retained six years; quarterly/annual board reporting on risks, methodologies and process effectiveness — [PwC on 2a-5/31a-4](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/library/sec-good-faith-determinations-of-fair-value.html)
- Valuation: policies must give more than "minimal guidance" and include procedures "designed to promote consistency" (Sciens) — [Cleary](https://www.clearyenforcementwatch.com/2026/03/enforcers-target-fund-valuation-practices/)
- Portfolio transactions and subscription/redemption orders recorded and kept five years (AIFMD Level 2 Arts. 64–66) — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2013/231/contents)
- Written communications relating to recommendations/advice preserved (Rule 204-2(a)(7)) — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/whatsapp-all-over-again-the-sec-brings-more-recordkeeping-charges-against-broker-dealers-and-investment-advisers-for-off-channel-communications)
- Allocation intent must be fixed at order entry, not after observing prices (Western Asset) — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/mutual-funds/western-asset-agrees-to-100m-sec-penalty-over-cherry-picking-scheme/266892)
- Model changes require controlled access and timely remediation of known vulnerabilities (Two Sigma) — [SEC 2025-15](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15)
- ESG process must be evidenced by training, testing, controls, standards, consistency and remediation — [CLS Blue Sky](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2023/10/19/arnold-porter-discusses-esg-whistleblowing-and-compliance-programs)
- Liquidity decisions need documented challenge and escalation — [FCA Dear CEO 2023](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/correspondence/dear-ceo-letter-liquidity-management-multi-firm-review.pdf)
- Hedge-fund memo baseline: thesis, variant view, model, catalysts, risks, sizing — [Daloopa](https://daloopa.com/blog/analyst-best-practices/hedge-fund-investment-memo-example)

### Inferences
Proposed "complete record" field list (synthesis; fields in italics are inferred from the frameworks rather than explicitly listed in any single source):
1. Identity: decision type, category, fund(s)/mandate(s) affected, owner (decider), timestamp of intent (pre-execution), version/seal hash.
2. Frame: question being decided, trigger/event, *time horizon*.
3. Inputs: data sources with as-of timestamps, model/methodology and version, third-party inputs (pricing vendor, proxy adviser, valuer), *base rate / reference class and precedents linked*.
4. Alternatives considered, including status quo, with reasons rejected.
5. Thesis / rationale; *expected value and distribution*; *confidence (probability)*; *catalysts*; *kill criteria and review date* (Duke/Mauboussin-derived).
6. Pre-mortem: top reasons this fails (Klein).
7. Policy evaluation: mandate/IPS rules checked, results, exceptions requested and their approvers.
8. Approvals: chain, each approver's independent view recorded before aggregation (MAP), weighted dissent (Bridgewater), quorum.
9. Action set committed (e.g., "Cash 70% / Scrip 30%") with deterministic per-fund implications.
10. Post-decision: outcome at review date, process-vs-outcome classification (Duke), lessons.
- For valuation and liquidity decisions the record should additionally capture committee composition/independence and conflicts declared, per FCA 2025 and 2023 reviews (inference from the review headings).

### Gaps
- No auditor (Big Four) checklist for investment-decision evidence was located; SOC 1 / ISAE 3402 control descriptions for asset managers would be the natural source and were not searched.
- The exact data elements of AIFMD Art. 64 (instrument identifier, quantity, price, counterparty, dates, person who took the decision, etc.) were not retrieved — the writer should quote the regulation text.

## Key Question 6: Where do current tools fail — evidence that decisions live in email/Excel/minutes/Slack, time-spent surveys, and behavioural-finance evidence on value-destroying decision types

### Takeaway
The behavioural evidence is strong and consistent: institutional PMs show skill in buying but destroy value in selling (Journal of Finance 2023), only 46% add value from sizing (Inalytics), and fewer than 40% of ranked active equity PMs added value through their decisions in Q4 2025 (Essentia). Evidence that decisions live in email/spreadsheets is real but weaker in quality — mostly vendor/sponsored surveys and regulator reviews — and the off-channel enforcement wave indirectly confirms that advice-related discussion happens in unretained messaging.

### Cited Findings

**Behavioural evidence by decision type**
- Akepanidtaworn, Di Mascio, Imas & Schmidt, "Selling Fast and Buying Slow: Heuristics and Trading Performance of Institutional Investors", *Journal of Finance* 78(6), 2023, pp. 3055–3098 (NBER WP 29076): "clear evidence of skill in buying"; selling decisions "underperform substantially — even relative to random selling strategies"; selling is a "fast", heuristic process triggered by extreme returns, whereas buying is slow and research-driven; portfolios in the dataset average $573m — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29076); [Wiley/JoF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13271); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3301277)
- Inalytics (DECSIS model; built from $22tn of trades over 20 years): four "alpha drivers" — stock picking, sizing, trading activity, holding periods; 88% of PMs demonstrated research skill (average research alpha 383 bps); alpha from buying was offset by poor selling "leaving the balance close to zero"; only 46% delivered positive alpha from sizing; sizing failures are "idiosyncratic" across PMs (per Di Mascio) — [MRM/Inalytics press](https://www.mrm-london.com/whats-happening/press/research-process-stock-picking-the-dominant-source-of-alpha-in-88-of-institutional-portfolios-finds-new-research-by-inalytics/); [Institutional Investor](https://institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1xp73qfbp3lnf/In-Active-Management-Alpha-Comes-Down-to-One-Thing); [IPE](https://www.ipe.com/inalytics-launches-manager-selection-analysis-tool-with-skills-based-data/10061188.article) (publication dates of the Inalytics figures not confirmed this session)
- Essentia Analytics Behavioral Alpha Benchmark, 31 Dec 2025 edition: median Behavioral Alpha Score of 163 PMs fell to 47.7 from 49.3 at 30 Sept 2025; only 19.0% of managers added value through half or more of their decisions; 50.3% had payoff ratios above 100% (56.4% in Q3); just 39.3% of portfolios added value through decision-making, down from 48.1% — [Essentia Q4 2025](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-q4-2025/)
- Essentia Q2 2025 edition: 135 active equity mutual-fund PMs, 1 Jul 2022–30 Jun 2025; score isolates skill from market conditions via hit rate (% of value-adding decisions) and payoff ratio (impact of good vs bad decisions) over 36 months — [Pzena/Essentia](https://www.pzena.com/americas/institutional-investors/about/newsroom/q2-2025-behavioral-alpha-benchmark-ranking-adding-value-essentia-analytics/); methodology paper — [Essentia white paper](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/behavioral-alpha-benchmark-paper/)
- Alpha Theory (vendor): optimal sizing beat actual in 11 of 12 years; 268% vs 130% compound — [Alpha Theory 2023 review](https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2023-year-in-review)
- Man Institute built a skill-measurement framework to give PMs timely feedback and studied fully committing to behavioural recommendations — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/commiting-to-behavioural-recommendations)
- Disposition effect (cutting winners, clinging to losers) traced to prospect theory; overconfidence, herding and recency identified as biases affecting PMs — [Springer review 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-024-00444-7); [Morgan Stanley IM](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_overcomingbehavioralbiases_us.pdf)

**Decisions living in email, spreadsheets, minutes**
- Sponsored article (Institutional Investor, 2025/26): 55% of asset owners still use spreadsheets for manual data consolidation, "wasting as much as a week every month"; firms managing privates are 20% more likely to use spreadsheets for portfolio management and valuations — survey sponsor and sample not identified in the snippet; treat as vendor-sourced — [Institutional Investor sponsored content](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/sponsored-content/hidden-margin-drain-why-manual-data-operations-are-becoming-too-expensive)

### Inferences
- The decision types with the best-evidenced value destruction (exits, sizing, adds/trims to losers) are precisely those with no regulatory record standard and, per Capital Group-style governance, no committee gate — so the product's case for them is performance feedback (journal + post-mortem scoring), whereas for valuation/liquidity/allocation/model changes the case is defensibility.
- The "selling is fast" finding suggests the record for an exit decision should be lightweight but mandatory (trigger, thesis-break vs. price move, replacement idea), because friction is the reason it is not captured today.
- Essentia's hit rate / payoff ratio construction is a ready-made scoring scheme for a decision ledger's outcome review.

- ValueExchange/DTCC corporate-actions survey statistics (share manual, error costs, election failures) were not retrievable.
