## Key Question 1 — United States: SEC/FINRA books-and-records, electronic record-keeping, Marketing Rule, PDA proposal status, AI exam focus, Regulation S-P, Form ADV, and what exams ask for

### Takeaway
For SEC-registered advisers the non-negotiable floor is Advisers Act Rule 204-2 (5-year retention, first 2 years in an appropriate office; electronic media must be safeguarded from alteration and reproducible on demand). There is no adviser-specific WORM rule; the broker-dealer Rule 17a-4 "audit-trail alternative" (2022 amendments, compliance 3 May 2023) is the de-facto technical benchmark buyers cite, and gnosis's append-only, time-stamped, identity-attributed event store maps directly onto it. The SEC's predictive-data-analytics conflicts proposal was formally withdrawn on 12 June 2025 [V]; AI supervision, AI-representation accuracy ("AI-washing") and cyber/vendor oversight are 2026 exam priorities [V]; Regulation S-P amendments took effect 3 Dec 2025 (larger) / 3 June 2026 (smaller) [V].

### Cited Findings

**Books and records — Advisers Act Rule 204-2**
- [V] The standard retention period under Rule 204-2 is five years, in an easily accessible place, with the first two years in an appropriate office of the investment adviser; some categories use a different trigger (e.g. dissemination of an advertisement, completion of annual compliance review, termination of the adviser). — [Global Relay: Advisers Act Rule 204-2 Explained](https://www.globalrelay.com/resources/the-compliance-hub/rules-and-regulations/advisers-act-rule-204-2-explained/); [Smarsh: Rule 204-2](https://www.smarsh.com/regulations/investment-advisers-act-of-1940-rule-204-2/)
- [P] Rule 204-2(a)(3) requires a memorandum of each order given for the purchase or sale of any security, showing terms and conditions, **identifying the person who recommended the transaction and the person who placed the order**, the account, date of entry and executing broker. This is the closest US analogue to a "decision record" and is the field set an examiner reconciles against the trade blotter. — [17 CFR 275.204-2 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-275/section-275.204-2) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Rule 204-2(a)(7) requires retention of written communications received and copies sent relating to any recommendation made or proposed, advice given or proposed, receipt/disbursement of funds or securities, and order placement. — same eCFR URL *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Rule 204-2(a)(17) requires retention of the compliance policies and procedures adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 and records documenting the annual review. — same eCFR URL *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Rule 204-2(g) (electronic storage media) requires the adviser to arrange and index records so they are readily accessible; promptly provide a legible, true and complete copy on request; separately store a duplicate; and establish procedures to **reasonably safeguard the records from loss, alteration or destruction**, limit access to authorised personnel and the Commission, and ensure reproductions are complete, true and legible. — same eCFR URL *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Rule 206(4)-7 (Compliance Rule, adopted Dec 2003, Release IA-2204) requires written policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent Advisers Act violations, an annual review, and a CCO; the adopting release lists "portfolio management processes, including allocation of investment opportunities among clients and consistency of portfolios with clients' investment objectives, disclosures by the adviser, and applicable regulatory restrictions" as an area the policies must address. — [SEC Release IA-2204](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/ia-2204.htm) *(not re-fetched)*

**Electronic record-keeping — Rule 17a-4 (broker-dealers) and the 2022 audit-trail alternative**
- [V] On 12 October 2022 the SEC adopted amendments to Rule 17a-4 adding an **audit-trail alternative** to WORM: an electronic recordkeeping system must "maintain and preserve electronic records in a manner that permits the recreation of an original record if it is modified or deleted" and must preserve records "in a way that maintains a complete time-stamped audit trail." WORM was retained as an option. Effective 3 January 2023; compliance date 3 May 2023 for broker-dealers. — [SEC: Amendments to Electronic Recordkeeping Requirements for Broker-Dealers](https://www.sec.gov/investment/amendments-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-broker-dealers); [Federal Register 2022-22670](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/11/03/2022-22670/electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-for-broker-dealers-security-based-swap-dealers-and-major); [Mayer Brown legal update, Oct 2022](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2022/10/legal-update--sec-adopts-amendments-to-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-for-brokerdealers-and-sbs-entities.pdf); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2022/10/sec-modernizes-broker-dealer-recordkeeping-requirements)
- [P] The audit-trail alternative (17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A)) requires the audit trail to include: all modifications to and deletions of a record or any part of it; the date and time of actions that create, modify or delete the record; if applicable, the identity of the individual(s) creating, modifying or deleting the record; and any other information needed to permit recreation of the original record and interim iterations. The 2022 amendments also replaced the "designated third party" (D3P) undertaking with an option to designate a senior executive officer (DEO) who can provide regulators access. — [17 CFR 240.17a-4 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/section-240.17a-4) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Investment Company Act Rule 31a-2 requires registered funds to preserve most records for **six years**, the first two in an easily accessible place. Rule 18f-4 (derivatives, compliance Aug 2022) adds record-keeping for the derivatives risk-management programme (5 years, first 2 easily accessible). — [17 CFR 270.31a-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.31a-2); [17 CFR 270.18f-4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-270/section-270.18f-4) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] CFTC Regulation 1.31 (amended 2017) removed its WORM requirement for CPO/CTA electronic records in favour of a technology-neutral standard of authenticity, reliability and ready accessibility. — [17 CFR 1.31](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-1/section-1.31) *(not re-fetched)*

**SEC Division of Examinations — 2026 priorities (AI)**
- [V] The 2026 Examination Priorities (Debevoise summary dated Nov 2025) highlight AI: examiners will assess whether firms have adequate policies and procedures to **monitor and/or supervise their use of AI**, whether firms' **actual AI usage matches representations** to clients and regulators (firms claiming to use AI for portfolio management must be able to demonstrate that AI tools genuinely influence investment decisions rather than serve as supplemental research), and AI-related cybersecurity and vendor-oversight controls. — [Debevoise: 2026 SEC Division of Examinations Priorities](https://www.debevoise.com/-/media/files/insights/publications/2025/11/2026-sec-division-of-examinations-priorities.pdf); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/alerts-privateequity-pif-2026-sec-exam-priorities-for-registered-investment-advisers); [Dorsey](https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2025/12/sec-2026-examination-priorities); [Plante Moran](https://www.plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/01/navigating-secs-2026-examination-priorities-for-investment-advisers); [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/sec-announces-2026-exam-priorities)

### Inferences
- [I] Because Rule 204-2 has no WORM/audit-trail specification of its own, buyers and their auditors borrow 17a-4(f)'s language. gnosis's append-only event store with identity- and time-stamped events satisfies the 17a-4 audit-trail alternative *if* (a) every mutation (including "sealed" commits and any admin action) is an event with actor identity and UTC timestamp, (b) the original and every interim iteration can be recreated (bitemporal replay does this), and (c) the store is tamper-evident (hash-chained or externally anchored) — 17a-4 does not mandate hash-chaining, but an auditor will ask how "cannot be altered" is demonstrated.
- [I] gnosis satisfies 204-2(a)(3)'s "who recommended / who placed" evidentiary need only if the *committed decision* record carries the recommending person(s), the approvers, and can be linked (by ID) to the OMS order memorandum. The decision itself is not the order; a gap exists unless there is an order-ID linkage.
- [I] 204-2(g)'s "separately store a duplicate copy" and "promptly provide a legible, true and complete copy" imply an export/reproduction path (e.g. signed PDF or JSON bundle of the as-of decision plus lineage) and geographically separate backup; neither is listed among gnosis's stated features.
- [I] The SEC 2026 priority on "actual AI usage matches representations" is a direct sales hook: gnosis's tool-call lineage is evidence that the AI did (or did not) influence a decision. Conversely, if the client's marketing says "AI-driven", the lineage becomes discoverable evidence — clients will want it retained under the Marketing Rule substantiation records (204-2(a)(16)).
- [I] What SEC examiners actually request (from typical initial document request lists, not citable to a public source): compliance manual and annual review; trade blotter and allocation records; investment guideline/restriction monitoring and **breach/exception logs with resolution**; investment committee minutes and supporting research; best-execution reviews; code-of-ethics/pre-clearance records; marketing pieces and substantiation; and since 2024 an inventory of AI tools, AI policies, and evidence of testing/supervision. gnosis's exception and approval records map to the breach/exception log request.

## Key Question 2 — United Kingdom: SYSC record-keeping, SM&CR evidence, Consumer Duty, FCA/PRA AI positions (2024-2026), PRA SS1/23, retention periods, Operational Resilience

### Takeaway
The UK floor is SYSC 9.1 (records "sufficient to enable the FCA to monitor compliance"; **at least 5 years for MiFID business**; records must be readily accessible, reconstitutable, and must show every change with the prior content) [V], plus SYSC 10 conflicts records and SM&CR's duty of responsibility. The FCA is explicitly not writing AI-specific rules and has said there will be **no dedicated AI Senior Manager Function** — accountability sits with existing SMFs, so "reasonable steps" evidence (audit trail, human oversight, model documentation) is what a senior manager needs [V]. PRA SS1/23 (effective 17 May 2024) applies to banks and PRA-designated investment firms with model permissions, **not to FCA-solo asset managers** [V], but is the reference standard clients borrow.

### Cited Findings

**Record-keeping — SYSC 9**
- [V] SYSC 9.1: a common platform firm must retain all records kept under the chapter in relation to its MiFID business for **at least five years** ("at least" is a floor — where another rule requires longer, the longer period wins; e.g. COBS 9A.4.3R / COBS 10A.7.2AR may require longer). For retention, the FCA "must be able to access the records readily, to **reconstitute each element** in a clear and accurate manner and to **identify easily any changes, corrections or other amendments, and the contents of the records prior to such modifications**." — [FCA Handbook SYSC 9.1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sysc9/sysc9s1); [SYSC 9 chapter](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sysc9); [GRIP summary](https://www.grip.globalrelay.com/rules/fca-sysc-9-1/); [Nasara Connect guide](https://nasaraconnect.com/blog/record-keeping-requirements-for-fca-firms-practical-guide-to-sysc-9)
- [P] SYSC 9.1.1R requires a firm to arrange for orderly records to be kept of its business and internal organisation, including all services and transactions, sufficient to enable the FCA to monitor compliance and in particular to ascertain that the firm has complied with all obligations to clients. Non-MiFID retention periods are set per sourcebook in each Schedule 1 record-keeping table (commonly 3 years for non-MiFID business; 5 years for MiFID; 6 years for certain insurance/pension records; indefinitely for pension-transfer advice records). — [FCA Handbook SYSC 9.1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/SYSC/9/1.html) *(not re-fetched; Schedule-1 periods are from prior knowledge and should be checked per record type)*
- [P] SYSC 10.1 (conflicts of interest): SYSC 10.1.3R requires identification of conflicts; SYSC 10.1.6R requires the firm to keep and regularly update a **record of the kinds of service or activity in which a conflict entailing a material risk of damage to clients has arisen or may arise**; SYSC 10.1.10R requires a written conflicts policy. SYSC 10.2 permits "Chinese walls" — arrangements requiring information held by one part of the business to be withheld from, or not used for, persons in another part (SYSC 10.2.2R). — [FCA Handbook SYSC 10](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/SYSC/10/) *(not re-fetched)*

**SM&CR — what a Senior Manager must evidence**
- [V] The FCA's position is that there will be **no dedicated SMF for AI**; responsibility for AI-driven outcomes sits within the existing SM&CR, and senior managers remain accountable if an AI vendor's system produces a flawed output leading to poor client outcomes. — [Addleshaw Goddard 2026: Can senior managers be liable for decisions made by AI?](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/global-investigations/senior-managers-liable-under-uk-regulatory-regime-decisions-made-ai/); [FCA: AI and the FCA — our approach](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach)
- [V] The House of Commons Treasury Committee report on AI in financial services recommended that **by the end of 2026 the FCA should publish practical guidance** on applying existing consumer-protection rules to AI and on "accountability and the level of assurance expected from senior managers under the SM&CR for harm caused through AI"; the Committee flagged bias, concentration risk and third-party dependency as live issues and signalled that guidance on audit trails and human-in-the-loop protocols is likely in 2026. It also called on the BoE/FCA to conduct AI-specific stress testing. — [Treasury Committee report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmtreasy/684/report.html); [Formiti summary](https://www.formiti.com/data-privacy-news/the-fca-s-ai-reckoning-how-uk-financial-services-can-navigate-the-2026-accountability-crisis)
- [V] BoE/FCA AI survey (Nov 2024): 75% of firms already using AI, a further 10% planning to within three years. — [Treasury Committee report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmtreasy/684/report.html)
- [V] In April 2026 the FCA and PRA confirmed changes to "streamline senior manager accountability and boost growth". — [Bank of England news, April 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2026/april/fca-and-pra-confirm-changes-to-streamline-senior-manager-accountability-and-boost-growth) *(content of the changes could not be opened; see Gaps)*
- [P] FSMA 2000 s.66A(5)/s.66B(5) (duty of responsibility): a Senior Manager is liable for a contravention in their area of responsibility unless they took "such steps as a person in the senior manager's position could reasonably be expected to take" to prevent it; DEPP 6.2.9-E lists factors the FCA considers, including whether the SM had adequate management information, documented the steps taken, and had systems and controls appropriate to the business. SYSC 24 (prescribed responsibilities) and SYSC 25 (responsibilities maps / statements of responsibilities) are the allocation records. — [FCA DEPP 6.2](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/DEPP/6/2.html) *(not re-fetched)*

**Consumer Duty**
- [P] PRIN 2A (Consumer Duty, PS22/9) applies to new/open products since 31 July 2023 and closed products since 31 July 2024; asset managers in a retail distribution chain are in scope as manufacturers; PRIN 2A.5 (consumer understanding) requires communications to be likely to be understood. — [FCA PS22/9](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps22-9-new-consumer-duty) *(not re-fetched)*

**FCA/PRA AI positions**
- [V] The FCA continues a principles-based approach using existing frameworks rather than AI-specific rules; the FCA AI Update (April 2024) is its baseline statement. — [FCA AI Update PDF](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/corporate/ai-update.pdf); [FCA AI approach page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach); [Hogan Lovells: new developments for AI in UK FS](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/new-developments-for-ai-in-uk-financial-services); [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/ai-regulation-in-financial-services-turning-principles-into-practice.html)

**PRA SS1/23 model risk management**
- [V] SS1/23 sets five principles — (1) model identification and model risk classification, (2) governance, (3) model development/implementation/use, (4) independent model validation, (5) model risk mitigants. In force **17 May 2024**. Applies to UK-incorporated banks, building societies and **PRA-designated investment firms with internal model approval** for regulatory capital (IRB, IMA or IMM) — i.e. not to FCA-solo-regulated asset managers. — [PRA SS1/23 page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2023/may/model-risk-management-principles-for-banks-ss); [SS1/23 PDF](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2023/ss123.pdf); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/regulatory-insights/pra-model-risk-management-principles.html); [UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/news-and-insight/blog/prudential-regulation-authority-pra-ss123-what-you-dont-know-can-hurt-you)
- [P] SS1/23's model definition explicitly covers AI/ML techniques and expects a model inventory, tiering, independent validation before use and after material change, and documentation sufficient for a third party to understand the model. — same SS1/23 PDF *(not re-fetched)*

**Operational Resilience and third parties**

## Key Question 3 — European Union: MiFID II record-keeping (Art 16, Delegated Regulation 2017/565, RTS 6), UCITS/AIFMD decision documentation and conflicts, DORA third-party obligations for a SaaS vendor, EU AI Act timing and scope, ESMA/EBA positions

### Takeaway
MiFID II Art 16(6)-(7) and Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art 72-76 set the EU floor: **5 years (up to 7 at NCA request)**, records in a medium that lets the NCA "reconstitute each key stage of the processing of each transaction", with corrections and prior contents ascertainable and no possibility of manipulation — and Art 74 requires a record of every **"decision to deal"** in portfolio management with the person who made it and the date/time [V]/[P]. AIFMD and UCITS Level 2 mirror these (5-year retention; recorded portfolio transactions; conflicts registers). DORA (applicable 17 Jan 2025) makes gnosis an "ICT third-party service provider" whose contract must carry Art 30 terms (data location, audit/access rights, incident assistance, exit). The EU AI Act's Annex III high-risk obligations were **deferred by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) to 2 December 2027** (Annex I to 2 Aug 2028); investment decision support is **not** an Annex III use case anyway; Art 50 transparency applied from 2 Aug 2026 and GPAI obligations from 2 Aug 2025 [V].

### Inferences
- [I] **Art 74 "decision to deal" is the single most important EU hook for gnosis**: MiFID portfolio managers (and by analogy AIFM/UCITS via Art 64 / Art 14) must record the person making the decision and the date/time immediately. gnosis's *commit* event is that record only if its schema carries the Annex IV Section 1 fields (client/fund designation, decision-maker, date-time to RTS 25 precision, instrument ID, buy/sell, quantity, price/type) or links unambiguously to the OMS record that does. Today gnosis captures decision + approvers; the field mapping and OMS link are a likely shortfall.
- [I] Art 72(1)(b)-(c) and Art 66(3) (AIFMD) are the textual basis for "point-in-time reconstruction": bitemporal replay satisfies them; a plain audit log that only shows diffs may not satisfy (b) "contents prior to such corrections ... easily ascertained" at scale.
- [I] Under DORA, gnosis is an ICT third-party provider; if a client classifies decision-recording as supporting a critical or important function (likely, since it is compliance evidence for portfolio management), the vendor must accept Art 30(3) terms: unrestricted audit/inspection rights, TLPT participation, incident assistance, exit/portability with a transition period, sub-contracting transparency, and data-location commitments. These are contract/operational items outside the product feature list; a SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 certificate is the customary evidence but does not replace the contractual rights.
- [I] EU AI Act: an investment decision-support/decision-ledger platform is **not high-risk** (not in Annex III) unless a client repurposes it for natural-person creditworthiness or life/health insurance pricing. Consequently Art 12 logging is not legally required of gnosis — but gnosis's per-tool-call lineage exceeds the Art 12/Art 26(6) six-month minimum and can be marketed as "high-risk-grade logging" — while noting financial-services record-keeping (5-7 years) governs retention anyway via Art 19(2)/26(6). Art 50(1) does apply to the chat agent if natural persons interact with it (inform them it is AI, unless obvious from context); Art 4 AI literacy applies to the client's staff.
- [I] The Digital Omnibus deferral reduces compulsion but not demand: ESMA's May 2024 statement and MiFID organisational requirements already require firms to understand, test, monitor and document AI used in portfolio management; the record-keeping hook is MiFID/AIFMD, not the AI Act.

## Key Question 4 — International standards: IOSCO (2021, 2025 consultation, 2026 final), FSB/BIS, model risk (SR 11-7 → SR 26-2; SS1/23), NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO 27001, SOC 1/SOC 2 Type II, ISAE 3402, GIPS

## Key Question 5 — What "point-in-time reconstruction" means to an auditor/regulator, and whether bitemporal replay is recognised as best practice

### Takeaway
Regulators define the *outcome*, not the technique: records must let the authority "reconstitute each key stage of the processing of each transaction" and see "any corrections... and the contents of the records prior to such corrections" (MiFID Delegated Reg Art 72(1); AIFMD L2 Art 66(3); UCITS Dir 2010/43 Art 16; FCA SYSC 9.1), and — in the US broker-dealer world — "permit the recreation of an original record if it is modified or deleted" with a complete time-stamped audit trail (Rule 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A), 2022) [V]. **No regulation or supervisory standard found names "bitemporal" as best practice**; the term appears in vendor and database literature (XTDB, Datomic-style systems) and in BCBS 239 lineage commentary from tooling vendors [V].

### Cited Findings
- [V] FCA SYSC 9.1: the FCA must be able to "reconstitute each element in a clear and accurate manner and to identify easily any changes, corrections or other amendments, and the contents of the records prior to such modifications." — [FCA Handbook SYSC 9.1](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sysc9/sysc9s1); [GRIP](https://www.grip.globalrelay.com/rules/fca-sysc-9-1/)
- [V] Rule 17a-4 audit-trail alternative: system must "permit the recreation of an original record if it is modified or deleted" and maintain "a complete time-stamped audit trail". — [SEC page](https://www.sec.gov/investment/amendments-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-broker-dealers); [Archive360 explainer](https://www.archive360.com/blog/sec-rule-17a-4-amended-taking-the-worm-requirement-out-of-our-misery); [ACA Group](https://www.acaglobal.com/industry-insights/amendments-rule-17a-4-electronic-recordkeeping-requirement/)
- [P] MiFID Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art 72(1)(a)-(c); AIFMD Delegated Regulation 231/2013 Art 66(3); UCITS Directive 2010/43/EU Art 16(3): "reconstitute each key stage of the processing of each [portfolio] transaction"; corrections and prior contents "easily ascertained"; "not possible for the records to be otherwise manipulated or altered". — EUR-Lex URLs as in KQ3 *(not re-fetched)*
- [V] Vendor/data-governance commentary on BCBS 239 lineage: supervisors "are asking to see lineage end-to-end on demand, and the answer needs to be a system query, not a three-week reconstruction project"; bitemporal queries let one "reconstruct the exact data state at filing time" and "identify when corrections were first recorded (system time)". These are vendor sources (DataHub, Atlan, Collibra, XTDB), not regulatory text. — [DataHub: BCBS 239 data lineage](https://datahub.com/blog/bcbs-239-data-lineage/); [Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/data-governance/bcbs-239-data-lineage/); [Collibra](https://www.collibra.com/blog/bcbs-239-explained-how-banks-can-prove-data-integrity-to-regulators); [XTDB: durable auditable orchestration](https://xtdb.com/discover/temporal-orchestration)
- [P] BCBS 239 Principle 3 (accuracy/integrity) and Principle 6 (adaptability) are the supervisory basis for "explain any number as of any date" in banks; the document does not mention bitemporality. — [BCBS 239](https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] RTS 25 (Delegated Regulation 2017/574) requires UTC-traceable, granular timestamps on reportable events — the practical standard an auditor uses to judge whether a "time-stamped audit trail" is trustworthy. — [Delegated Regulation 2017/574](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0574) *(not re-fetched)*

### Inferences
- [I] To an auditor, "point-in-time reconstruction" means three concrete tests: (1) *state test* — show me the decision, its inputs, the policy version and the approvals exactly as the firm saw them at time T (valid-time); (2) *knowledge test* — show me what the firm *knew* at T versus what was later corrected (transaction-time), with who corrected it and when; (3) *integrity test* — prove nothing was altered since (hash chain / WORM / independent attestation). Bitemporal replay answers (1) and (2) natively; (3) requires cryptographic or storage-level evidence plus a SOC 2 Type II processing-integrity opinion — gnosis's stated feature list covers (1)-(2) and only implicitly (3).
- [I] Because no regulator names bitemporality, the sales claim should be "we meet MiFID Art 72(1)(b)-(c) / SYSC 9.1 / 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) by construction", with bitemporality described as the mechanism, not the standard.
- [I] Bitemporal replay also depends on versioned reference data (policy/mandate versions, restricted lists, model versions, prompt templates). If gnosis replays decisions against *current* policy text rather than the policy version in force at T, the reconstruction fails the state test.

### Gaps
- No audit-standard text (ISA 500/315, PCAOB AS 1105) or regulator document explicitly describing bitemporal storage could be located; the claim that bitemporality is "recognised best practice" is unsupported by any authority found.
- ECB's 2024 RDARR guide and BoE "transforming data collection" materials could not be checked.

## Key Question 6 — Segregation of duties and four-eyes: which rules require dual approval for guideline exceptions, and what evidence must be retained

### Takeaway
I found **no US, UK or EU rule that expressly mandates two-person approval of an investment-guideline exception at transaction level**. "Four eyes" is a legal requirement at *entity-governance* level (two persons effectively directing the business: MiFID II Art 9(6), AIFMD Art 8(1)(c), UCITS Art 7(1)(b), CRD Art 13) and, in some fund domiciles (Luxembourg, Malta), a supervisory expectation extended to fund-level decisions [V]. The *evidence* standard for whatever approval process a firm adopts is set by the record-keeping rules: approver identity, date/time, and pre/post contents (17a-4 audit-trail; MiFID Art 72; SYSC 9.1).

### Cited Findings
- [V] The four-eyes principle is "a requirement that two individuals must approve an action before it can be taken"; in investment management, "portfolio managers often collaborate with compliance officers to approve significant investment decisions"; audit evidence "should show both the maker action and checker decision" with all requests and decisions logged. — [ChequeDB: four-eyes foundations](https://chequedb.com/resources/blog/four-eyes-principle-foundations); [Flagsmith](https://www.flagsmith.com/blog/what-is-the-four-eyes-principle); [Decisions.com](https://decisions.com/blog/2-eyes-4-eyes-6-eyes-principle)
- [V] Fund-domicile supervisors have extended the four-eyes principle to private funds (Waystone on Luxembourg/Ireland-type requirements) and Malta's MFSA treats it as a requirement for regulated companies. — [Waystone: Extension of four eyes principle to private funds](https://www.waystone.com/extension-of-four-eyes-principle-to-private-funds/); [Mondaq (Malta)](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1559702/what-is-the-four-eyes-principle-that-regulated-companies-need-to-adhere-to); [MK Fintech Partners](https://mkfintechpartners.com/2024/12/16/what-is-the-four-eyes-principle-that-regulated-companies-need-to-adhere-to/)
- [P] Entity-level "two persons" requirements: MiFID II Art 9(6) (at least two persons effectively direct the business); AIFMD Art 8(1)(c); UCITS Directive Art 7(1)(b); CRD IV Art 13(1). — EUR-Lex URLs as above *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Independence/segregation requirements that generate approval steps: AIFMD L2 Art 42 (functional and hierarchical separation of risk management from operating units) and Art 61 (independent compliance); UCITS Dir 2010/43 Art 12 (independent risk management); MiFID Delegated Reg Art 22-23 (compliance and risk independence); SEC Rule 206(4)-7 (compliance policies covering portfolio management/restrictions); FCA SYSC 6.1 and 7.1. RTS 6 Art 5-8 require documented sign-off before deploying or materially changing a trading algorithm. — *(not re-fetched)*
- [V] Rule 17a-4's audit-trail alternative requires the identity of the individual and date/time of every create/modify/delete action — the minimum evidence for any approval. — [SEC page](https://www.sec.gov/investment/amendments-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-broker-dealers)

### Inferences
- [I] gnosis's chained maker-checker approvals and non-overridable hard gates **exceed** any explicit legal requirement for buy-side guideline exceptions; the sales framing should be "your policies (206(4)-7 / SYSC 6 / AIFMD Art 61) promise segregation — gnosis makes it un-bypassable and self-evidencing", not "the law requires four eyes".
- [I] The minimum evidence set an auditor will test for each exception: requester identity; the specific rule/limit breached and the value at the time; the pre-trade vs post-trade classification; approver identities and their independence from the requester (role at time T); UTC timestamps; free-text rationale; expiry/remediation; and immutability. gnosis's phases (request exception → approvals → commit) cover these if rationale is mandatory and role membership is versioned.
- [I] Luxembourg (CSSF Circular 18/698) and Ireland (CBI fund management company guidance) practices mean EU fund clients will ask for conducting-officer / designated-person sign-off evidence; gnosis's approval chains should support role-typed approvers.

- No regulator document found that specifies "approver identity, timestamp, rationale" as an explicit triad for guideline exceptions; the triad is derived from general record-keeping rules.

## Key Question 7 — Information barriers and conflicts of interest (MAR, Advisers Act §204A, FCA SYSC 10): what a "hard gate" must demonstrably do

### Takeaway
The legal design target for gnosis's non-overridable information-barrier gate is **MAR Art 9(1)(a)**: the firm is protected only if its arrangements "effectively ensure" that neither the decision-maker nor anyone who influenced the decision possessed the inside information [P]. The gate must therefore be able to *prove* who could see what, at decision time, that the restricted/insider status was in force, and that no override occurred — plus produce the SYSC 10.1.6R / MiFID Art 35 / AIFMD Art 35 conflicts record.

### Cited Findings
- [P] MAR Art 9(1)(a) — legitimate behaviour where the legal person "has established, implemented and maintained adequate and effective internal arrangements and procedures that effectively ensure that neither the natural person who made the decision ... nor any other natural person who may have had any influence on that decision, was in possession of the inside information"; Art 18 insider lists (retained 5 years, immediately available to the NCA, with timestamps of when each person obtained access). — [Regulation (EU) 596/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] Advisers Act §204A: written policies reasonably designed to prevent misuse of MNPI; SEC enforcement historically tests whether the adviser had restricted lists, wall-crossing logs and surveillance. — [SEC codes-of-ethics release page](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2004/07/investment-adviser-codes-ethics)
- [P] FCA SYSC 10.2.2R (Chinese walls) and SYSC 10.1.6R (conflicts record), SYSC 10.1.10R (conflicts policy). — [FCA Handbook SYSC 10](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/SYSC/10/) *(not re-fetched)*
- [P] MiFID Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art 34(3): conflicts procedures must include "effective procedures to prevent or control the exchange of information between relevant persons"; Art 35: conflicts record. AIFMD L2 Art 30-35 and UCITS Dir 2010/43 Art 17-20 mirror this. — EUR-Lex URLs as above *(not re-fetched)*

### Inferences
- [I] A "hard gate" is demonstrable only if the ledger records, for each decision: the barrier/restricted-list version in force at T; the evaluation result; the identities of everyone who *could* influence the decision (proposer, input-capturers, approvers) and their wall status at T; and an immutable record that no admin override path exists (negative evidence — e.g. an admin audit log showing no privilege escalation, and code-level attestation in the SOC 2 report). gnosis's "admins cannot override" is a strong claim; auditors will ask for the control-test evidence behind it, not just the assertion.
- [I] gnosis does not itself maintain MAR Art 18 insider lists; the gate must ingest the client's insider/restricted list system (or provide one) — a likely integration gap.

## Key Question 8 — Explainability: do regulators require a reproducible rationale for an AI recommendation, and does anyone restrict unsupported causal language?

### Takeaway
No regulator found requires that an AI recommendation's rationale be *reproducible* for buy-side internal decision support. Binding explainability duties exist only for EU AI Act high-risk systems (Art 13, 14, 86 — deferred to Dec 2027 and not applicable to investment decision support) and for GDPR Art 22 solely-automated decisions affecting natural persons. What *is* binding is substantiation and "clear, fair, not misleading": SEC Marketing Rule 206(4)-1(a)(2) (must be able to substantiate material statements of fact on demand — the Global Predictions case failed exactly this) [V], FCA COBS 4.2.1R, MiFID II Art 24(3), and ESMA's May 2024 expectation that firms are transparent about AI's role and present it in a clear, fair and not-misleading way [V]. **No rule explicitly restricts "unsupported causal verbs"**; gnosis's prose validator is a voluntary control that operationalises substantiation.
